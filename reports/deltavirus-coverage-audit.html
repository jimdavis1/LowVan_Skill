Does the hepatitis delta virus module actually work?

Hepatitis delta virus needs hepatitis B to make a particle, infects an estimated 12 million people, and carries the fastest progression to cirrhosis of any chronic viral hepatitis. Its 1.7 kb circular RNA encodes one ORF that makes two proteins: the small antigen stops at an amber codon, and ADAR1 edits that codon so the large antigen runs 19 residues further. This is the audit of the module built for it — what it covers, whether genomes reach it, whether it calls both proteins, and what it refuses to call.

01What the module covers

One module for hepatitis delta virus, and a second for the rest of the family — that split was measured, not assumed.

1,176 hepatitis delta virus genomes, covered by 6 reference contigs. The divergent kolmiovirus genera — snake, rodent, bat, bird, fish, newt, toad and termite agents — are annotated in a separate Kolmioviridae module. They make a single antigen, not two, and that antigen sits on the same locus as the small antigen here.

The two cannot be separated by score. Across 660 exemplars the divergent profile scored 35–442 bits on hepatitis delta virus against 157–432 on its own genomes — fully interleaved, with no gap to put a threshold in. Inside one module that produced three calls on 601 of 660 genomes. Rep-contig routing does separate them, 659 of 659, so the family is partitioned by reference set instead. After the split, no genome in either module calls the other module's feature.

02The edited codon

One codon decides which protein the genome makes, and the deposits disagree about what is in it.

The small antigen ends at an amber codon (UAG). ADAR1 edits it to UGG, and the large antigen continues 19 residues to the CXXQ farnesylation box at Cys211 that lets it package virions. Both forms are made by every infected cell, so both are called on every genome — whatever that codon happens to hold in the deposit. Of the 852-member large-antigen collection, 508 carry the unedited TAG, 392 the IUPAC ambiguity code TRG, and 143 the edited TGG.

TRG is physical evidence of a mixed edited and unedited population in the sample. It translates as X and it is not a stop, so it reaches the terminator by the ordinary path and internal_stop never sees it. That is why the module declares a second, independent mechanism.

transl_except names the residue to replace, what to put there, and where to look — measured on the emitted protein, so the JSON never learns a profile name:
{"replace_residue": ["X","*"], "insert_residue": "W", "offset": [19,20], "from": "C-term"}

The window is 19–20 rather than a single number because genotype III's extension is genuinely one residue longer, with a different editing RNA structure behind it (PMID 12097551). It is tight on purpose. Across all 852 members, every one resolves to exactly one position in that window; widening it by one residue on each side would make 2 ambiguous, and by two would make 4. Where more than one candidate residue falls in the window the exception is declined and logged rather than guessed, because two ambiguities there is a sequencing problem and silently picking one would bury it.

In production the annotator says so in its own trace: L-HDAg transl_except: residue 196 of 214 translated as *; emitted as W.

Eight genomes are declined, and that is the correct answer. They route perfectly well — 1,159 to 1,756 bits — but seven carry an antigen ORF with 2 to 4 internal stop codons. The translation is unmistakably a delta antigen (MSRSESRKNRGGREDILEQWVSGRKRL…) but coverage before the first stop falls to 0.07–0.49, under the 0.65 floor. The eighth is 27% N. The module refuses to invent a protein from a disrupted reading frame.

04Where the thresholds sit

Measured on the annotator's own per-hit scores, not on the proteins it finally emits.

At the generated cutoff of 30, 29 genomes carried a duplicate call — the same feature twice, from a second weaker HSP. A length or coverage threshold trades those away one-for-one against genuine calls, so it is the wrong instrument. The trace gave a clean separation instead.

That took duplicates from 29 genomes to 8. The eight that remain are a property of the deposits, not of the profiles: four (AF01–AF04) carry the antigen at full length on both strands, scoring 481 and 468, which no score can separate. They are reported as poor-quality genomes rather than tuned away.

The ambiguity flag is expected for this virus and should not be read as noise: the editing site itself is deposited as TRG in 392 genomes, because the sample held both populations. These are clinical quasispecies deposits, and the flag is recording that honestly.

06What was missed

The limits, stated plainly.

Both profiles recover 100% of their own collections (852/852 and 685/685), so nothing in the curated set is uncallable. 24 distinct BV-BRC annotation strings collapse onto the 2 controlled names, a 12-fold reduction.

Nothing is declared without a profile. Of the 1,651 deposits that fail the length windows, 1,594 are partial entries of 150 aa or less whose C-termini are complete — fragments of the two modelled forms, not a third product nobody has modelled.

The strings are wrong in both directions and nothing here was binned on one: 21 deposits labelled “small…antigen” are 214 aa, 94 fragments sit under the large label, and 71 genuine long forms say neither. Every sequence was placed by length and by the chemistry at the editing site.

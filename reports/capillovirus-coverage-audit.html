Does the apple stem grooving virus module actually work?

Capillovirus is a genus of the Betaflexiviridae: apple stem grooving virus, cherry virus A, citrus tatter leaf virus and about thirty more, mostly of woody perennials. One genome layout throughout — a ~241 kDa ORF1 polyprotein carrying the coat protein at its C-terminus, and a 36 kDa ORF2 movement protein expressed from a subgenomic RNA. Three features, and the module calls all three on every genome it routes.

01What the module covers

One genus, one layout, so the partitioning question never arose.

A LowVan module is defined by genome organisation rather than phylogeny. Capillovirus needed no decision: every member carries the same two ORFs, so the genus and the module are the same set. That is unusual in this build — Quinvirinae, Bromoviridae and Tymoviridae all had to be split or lumped against measurement.

The coat protein is inside ORF1 as well as being expressed on its own. That is visible in the source data: of 56 records annotated as a coat protein, 11 begin somewhere other than a methionine — 8 of them at isoleucine at exactly 205 aa — because they were annotated as a segment of the polyprotein. Those are excluded from the CP collection so the profile starts at the subgenomic Met.

02How it was built

779 unique proteins, 97.0% of features binned, and one string that named the wrong enzyme.

Three source labels turned out to be the same protein. Representatives of serine protease, 36K protein and putative movement protein are 96.9–98.4% identical to one another over their full length (blastp, E=0.0) and are all ORF2. The protease label is simply wrong: capilloviruses encode a papain-like cysteine protease, and it is a domain of ORF1, not a 320 aa standalone. Binning on the word protease would have created a 25-sequence phantom feature out of MP's own sequences.

serine protease       vs putative movement protein   96.9%  E=0.0   642 bits
36K protein           vs putative movement protein   98.4%  E=0.0   648 bits
36K protein           vs serine protease             96.9%  E=0.0   639 bits
movement protein      vs 36K protein                 42.5%/212      188 bits

The last row is the other finding. MP is bimodal: a 320 aa class and a 463 aa class, 42% identical over 212 residues — divergent but genuinely the same ORF2 homology group. The default length window is 0.7–1.3× the feature's median, the pooled median is 320, and that window is 224–416. Every one of the 119 sequences in the larger class would have been discarded as overlong, with every number in the report self-consistent. MP was given an explicit 260–480 window instead.

04Does it route?

Yes — completely, and on half the reference budget.

Routing is a BLASTn match against a set of reference contigs. The project budget is 25 references per module; a taxon that cannot reach most of its genomes inside that budget is tabled rather than forced.

07What it cannot do

Two real limits, both deliberate.

No mature peptides. ORF1 is a polyprotein and its cleavage sites are not documented in a form that can be read off the sequence for this genus. The project rule is that a mature peptide is declared only when the literature gives coordinates or a cleavage motif; guessing from a homologue's sites is how a module ships a feature it cannot actually locate. So the polyprotein is called whole and its products are not claimed.

ORF1 domain labels are not features. 26 features in the source are annotated RNA-dependent RNA polymerase core region, every one of them exactly 270 aa. That is a Pfam domain footprint on ORF1, not a separately encoded protein, and declaring it would produce a feature no genome actually has.

The evaluation is a sample. 150 of 334 genomes. Whole-taxon reach is the routing figure in §04, which is a census; the call and quality rates in §05 and §06 are estimates from the sample, precise to roughly ±6 points at n=150.

08What the run found

One mislabelled protein and one length trap, both caught before shipping.

The serine protease label would have cost MP a quarter of its sequences and created a feature for an enzyme the genus does not encode in that form. The bimodal MP length distribution would have cost it another 119. Neither would have produced an error message; both were found by measuring rather than by reading the source's words.

Nothing outstanding. The module calls three of three features on every routed genome, and both defects found during the build were fixed before it shipped.

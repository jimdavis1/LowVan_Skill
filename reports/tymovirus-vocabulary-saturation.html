Tymovirus

A controlled vocabulary stops growing. Free text does not.

A Tymovirus genome encodes the same 3 modelled proteins as any other, so a curated vocabulary needs exactly 3 names no matter how many genomes you read. Free-text product strings have no such ceiling: each new submitter spells the same proteins a new way. Sampling 97 annotated genomes 100 times over shows the two curves doing exactly that — the module is saturated after one genome, while the source vocabulary is still climbing at 97.

The ratio is the number worth quoting: at the end of each curve, free text costs 44.3 strings per 100 genomes and the controlled vocabulary 3.1 — a 14.3-fold collapse. Reaching 95% of its final size takes the source vocabulary 79 genomes (81% of the set); the module gets there on genome 1.

01What the module covers

Two genera that share a layout, and the one gene that defines it.

The module covers Bocaparvovirus and Aveparvovirus. They are held together because the layout is the same and their peripheral profiles do not cross-match, which is the test for whether a set of genera is one module or several.

Five features are declared: NS1, NP1, the capsid pair VP1 and VP2, and ORFX. NS1, NP1 and VP1 carry copy_num.

NP1 is what makes a bocaparvovirus a bocaparvovirus: a third gene, between the nonstructural and capsid genes, that no other parvovirus genus has. It is given a function — viral RNA processing protein — because that function is established in the literature, not inferred from position.

Eighty distinct annotation strings collapse onto the five feature names, over 6,195 source sequences — the largest collection in the family. NS1 draws on twenty-two strings, VP1 on twenty-one, VP2 on eighteen, NP1 on sixteen.

NP1's sixteen are the ones worth reading. NP1 and NS2 are slots in a genome layout, not names of proteins, and one cluster in this build arrived carrying both labels. The binning is by sequence, not by the slot a deposit was filed under.

Only three strings did not collapse, three sequences in all: structural protein 3, Non-structural ORFs and Structural ORFs. The last two are not protein names at all.

Routing was measured against the 25 reference contigs this module ships, of which 25 were used by the exemplar set.

Of 74 exemplars, 73 routed to this module and 1 did not route at all. None went to another module.

One unrouted genome out of 74 is a divergent isolate with no close relative among the 25 reference contigs. Routing is nucleotide identity, so a genome with no near neighbour is invisible to the module no matter how recognisable its proteins would be.

07What it cannot do

Three limits, each measured rather than supposed.

VP1 and VP2 are still called at identical coordinates in 14 of 299 genomes. The capsid rebuild took the family-wide figure down by two orders of magnitude but did not reach zero here. Fourteen genomes have a VP1 call that is actually VP2's open reading frame, and the module does not currently distinguish them.

ORFX is declared on five sequences. Its bit floor of 233 is high enough that it will rarely fire falsely, and low enough coverage that it will rarely fire at all. Do not read its absence as information.

No splice features are declared. Bocaparvovirus transcription is spliced and NP1 is involved in processing those messages, but every feature here is called from a profile built on deposited protein. A spliced product that was never deposited would be invisible.

Use this module for bocaparvovirus and aveparvovirus genomes; routing and calling are both strong and the panel is the cleanest in the family. Check VP1 and VP2 coordinates before trusting a VP1 call: in 14 of 299 genomes they are the same.

08What the run found

What the build turned up that was not expected.

A single cluster arrived labelled both NP1 and NS2. Those are positions in a genome, not protein names, and different submitters file different proteins under each. Had the build trusted the labels it would have split one protein into two features, or merged two into one.

The capsid labels were wrong here as they were across the family, just less severely. The nested VP forms were rebinned by size per species rather than by the name on the deposit, and the profiles rebuilt; 14 genomes still show the collision, down from a far larger number before the rebuild.

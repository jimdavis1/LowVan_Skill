Carlavirus

A controlled vocabulary stops growing. Free text does not.

A Carlavirus genome encodes the same 6 modelled proteins as any other, so a curated vocabulary needs exactly 6 names no matter how many genomes you read. Free-text product strings have no such ceiling: each new submitter spells the same proteins a new way. Sampling 148 annotated genomes 100 times over shows the two curves doing exactly that — the module is saturated after 2 genomes, while the source vocabulary is still climbing at 148.

6strings, controlled vocabulary

The ratio is the number worth quoting: at the end of each curve, free text costs 71.6 strings per 100 genomes and the controlled vocabulary 4.1 — a 17.7-fold collapse. Reaching 95% of its final size takes the source vocabulary 133 genomes (90% of the set); the module gets there on genome 2.

01What the module covers

One family, two segments, five features — and a capsid that cuts itself in half.

The module covers Picobirnaviridae, a family of bisegmented double-stranded RNA viruses found in the gut of essentially every vertebrate that has been looked at, and in invertebrates and environmental samples besides. Segment 2 carries the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. Segment 1 carries the capsid precursor and, in many lineages, a second uncharacterised open reading frame upstream of it.

The capsid is the interesting part. In rabbit picobirnavirus the precursor cleaves itself, intra-dimer and autocatalytically, C-terminal to a conserved Asn65, giving a ~7 kDa N-terminal peptide and the ~55 kDa mature capsid (PMID 19407816, PDB 2VF1). Mutating that asparagine abolishes the reaction. The module therefore ships three capsid features — the precursor as a CDS, and both cleavage products as mat_peptides — rather than flattening them into one call.

The fifth feature, ORF1, is a genuine lineage-specific protein with no assigned function. It is named Uncharacterized lineage-specific protein rather than given a mass or a position label, because ‘ORF1’ is a coordinate in a genome, not a homology group.

The source collections are RDRP 3,888, CP 1,062 and ORF1 393 collected sequences, of which 1,488, 590 and 377 fall inside the declared length window and are what the profiles are built from. The difference is length-outliers, which are kept as evidence and counted in the table below but never clustered. Neither cleavage product is deposited anywhere, so neither could be collected: CP_MAT and CP_NP (418 sequences each) were cut out of the CP collection at the autoproteolysis site.

The cut is made by motif rather than by alignment column, because a column is true of one clustering only. Measured over the 500 CP members whose alignment to the paper's own rabbit PBV capsid reaches residue 65, the junction chemistry is enzyme-determined: P1 Asn 95%, P1′ Asp 87%, P4′ Trp 88%, P5′ Tyr 92%. The pattern N | x x x W Y occurs exactly once in the first 140 residues of 427 of 589 members and more than once in two — specific, not merely present.

The documented path was tried first and refused the job. apply_signalp.py --motif builds a 60-residue consensus per cluster and rejected three clusters with ‘cut 64/66 beyond the 60 consensus residues’. This cleavage sits at residue 45–66, past where a signal peptide would be. The derivation was done in a dedicated script with the measurement recorded, rather than widening the tool's defaults silently.

Profile building needed non-default mutual-information floors, one per feature, and the reason was measured rather than guessed. At the documented -mi 0.8 the ORF1 collection forms 350 clusters and not one reaches five members, so the feature builds zero profiles. Cluster coverage at ≥5 members: ORF1 0% at 0.8 and 43% at 0.3; CP 2% at 0.8 and 77% at 0.3; RDRP 27% at 0.8 and 81% at 0.6. Each feature was lowered only as far as it needed — RDRP 0.6, CP and the two products 0.35, ORF1 0.3 — not to a common value.

The floors are ORF1 30, CP_MAT 60, CP 80 and RDRP 300 bits, all at 0.65 coverage. 157 profiles were built: RDRP 108, CP 19, CP_MAT 16, ORF1 14.

CP_NP has no profile and no bit floor at all. It is a non_pssm_partner — it is called as the remainder upstream of CP_MAT, so a cutoff would mean nothing for it. Its length bounds are the one place this module's JSON cannot be generated correctly: call_non_pssm_features compares a contig coordinate span to min_len/max_len, while the generator measures those from the collection in residues. Left alone it shipped 18–137, which no 54-residue peptide could ever satisfy. The bounds are rewritten to 54–400 nt by a post-generation script so that a regeneration reproduces the fix instead of losing it.

Self-recall against each feature's own in-range collection: 2835 of 2873 (98.7%) — RDRP 1470/1488, ORF1 358/377, CP 589/590, CP_MAT 418/418. This is a floor, not a validation; the genome-level figures are in §05 and §06.

04Does it route?

Routing is a BLASTn of the query against the reference contigs, and in this family the two segments behave completely differently.

Routing was measured against the 25 reference contigs this module ships, of which 24 were used by the exemplar set. Exemplars are the 3,100 contigs that survive clustering the 5,920-contig dump at 95% identity with a 1,100 nt floor.

Split by segment — the only way the headline figure means anything — segment 2 routes at 83.7% (1,376 of 1,643) and segment 1 at 16.5% (232 of 1,408). Feature calls follow exactly: RdRp 1,380, CP and CP_MAT 211 each, CP_NP 194, ORF1 199. Only 4 exemplars in 3,100 went to another module at all.

The merge rule is what rescues segment 1. Measured on the 83 genome names that deposit both segments: presented as a merged whole genome they route at 84.3%; their capsid contigs presented alone route at 13.5%. Once a genome routes, the capsid is called by profile search, which does not care that the nucleotide sequence is unalignable. The held-out whole-genome panel agrees independently at 87%.

So the 47.7% unrouted figure above is an artifact of auditing loose contigs. It is the honest way to audit and it is reported unrounded, but the module's real-world unit is the merged genome, and that is where it performs.

Three things were ruled out by measurement, not by argument. It is not reference selection: an all-vs-all of the exemplars allows the 25 references to be chosen by greedy set cover, and the hand-picked set (16.4% / 83.7%) is within 1.6 points of the best any 25 files can do (17.0% / 85.5%). It is not the absence of whole genomes: only 101 of 5,860 genome names in this dump carry both segments, and 25 references chosen from all 101 route less segment 1 (10.9%), with a ceiling of 14.1% if you use every one of them. And it is not a coverable gap: all-vs-all among the 1,408 capsid exemplars finds 488 that hit nothing but themselves, and the greedy curve never saturates — 20 references 20.7%, 40 references 29.8%, 60 references 36.3%, still climbing about five genomes per reference.

Picobirnavirus segment 1 simply has no nucleotide-level core to cluster on. Reoviridae and Orthomyxoviridae do not hit this, because their segments are routinely deposited together.

07What it cannot do

Four limits, each measured rather than supposed.

A standalone capsid segment routes 16.4% of the time, and that was left as it is. The binding constraint is the routing floor: -mcb defaults to 150 bits. Swept against the family routing repo, segment 1 would go to 21.0% at 100, 40.6% at 60 and 72.3% at 40, with segment 2 also improving to 90.9%, at a cost of 10 misroutes in 3,100 instead of 4. The cliff below that is sharp — at mcb 30 misrouting jumps to 81.

But -mcb is a global command-line flag with no per-module field: the JSON block carries only close_genomes and segments, so this family cannot declare the floor it needs, and lowering it globally would change every other module in the same run. The decision was taken to leave the floor at 150 and ship as measured. The roughly 1,180 standalone capsid records that would become annotatable at mcb 40 are a known, quantified gap recorded here rather than bought with a change to everyone else's results.

Two genomes in the panel fail RdRp for a reason no profile can fix: they are gcode 6 records, where TAA and TAG encode glutamine. They are the only RdRp failures on segment 2 in the whole panel.

Processing has been demonstrated in rabbit picobirnavirus, not across the family. Human PBV capsid expressed in E. coli is not processed (PMID 32938763), and that paper's ‘Δ45 CP’ is an engineered truncation, not a natural product. The two cleavage products ship because the Asn is conserved at 93% family-wide, not because cleavage has been shown outside rabbit PBV. Treat CP_MAT and CP_NP as predicted boundaries with a measured motif behind them.

Use this module on merged whole genomes, where routing is 84–87% and all five features call. A lone RdRp segment is also fine. A lone capsid segment will usually not route at the shipped floor, and that is a property of picobirnavirus segment 1 rather than of this build — no reference budget fixes it, and the one thing that would is a global change this module declined to make.

08What the run found

What the build turned up that was not expected.

A deposited RdRp that is a capsid. fig|442302.297.peg.1 is deposited as RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. It blasts to CP at 154 bits over 412 residues and to RDRP at 27 bits — 549 aa with a Lys/Asn-rich capsid N-terminus. It was moved RDRP→CP in both collections. The deposited name is a submitter's claim like any other field, and it is the one field nothing downstream checks.

Twenty-one CP members that also pass as CP_MAT. Checked by hand, every one is a full-length Met-initiated precursor that carries the cut motif. That is architecture, not contamination, and it is why the two features are allowed to overlap.

A quality-control script that was reporting on the wrong thing. check_rep_contigs.py reported 44 of 190 genomes routed. It calls blastn with no search parameters, so it runs megablast — word size 28, reward 1 — while the annotator runs word size 11 with reward 2 and penalty −3. KC692366 came back NO BLASTn HIT; at the annotator's own settings it hits a reference at 514 bits. The correct whole-genome figure is 77 of 89. Any routing number produced by a kit script that calls bare blastn is low by roughly four-fold.

A trap in the reference selection itself. Choosing references by greedy cover over genome names reports segment 1 at 39.0% — a large and entirely false gain. ‘Marmot picobirnavirus’ is one name covering 298 separate contigs, and treating a name as a reference file builds a 298-contig mega-reference the budget does not permit. Exactly one name in the whole dump has more than three exemplar contigs, which is why the error is easy to miss.

Rarefaction over proteins: every annotated protein in the 1,422 genomes both sources annotate (1,490 source product records, 1,660 module calls) shuffled 100 times and drawn one at a time. Both curves start at zero. The source vocabulary reaches 20 distinct strings, the module's 11. At the depth both reach, 1,490 proteins, free text has used 20.0 strings against 10.9 — a 1.8-fold difference. The source reaches 95% of its final size after 1,093 proteins, the module after 1,084.

Across 1,422 genomes annotated by both the source and this module, 1,490 deposited proteins carry 20 distinct annotation strings; the module's calls carry 11, of which five are its own feature names and the rest are the no-profile placeholder and the four known misrouted genomes. At equal depth that is a 1.8-fold collapse.

That is a smaller reduction than most modules show, and the reason is worth stating plainly: picobirnavirus deposits are already terse. The gain here is not in shrinking a sprawling vocabulary but in adding structure — three capsid features where the source has one string, and a named uncharacterised ORF where the source has none.

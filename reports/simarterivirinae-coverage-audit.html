01What the module covers

A second module exists for one reason: these genomes carry the minor-glycoprotein cassette twice.

The module covers Simarterivirinae, the simian arteriviruses — simian haemorrhagic fever virus and its relatives in African and Asian primates. They are split from the rest of Arteriviridae on genome organisation, not phylogeny, and the organisation in question is a duplicated cassette: where every other arterivirus carries one set of minor envelope glycoproteins, these carry two (PMID 25036340).

That gives 14 features. Nine are the ordinary arterivirus set — ORF1A, GP2, E, GP3, GP4, ORF5A, GP5, M, N — plus ORF1AB from the −1 ribosomal frameshift. The remaining four are the duplicate copies: EP, GP2P, GP3P and GP4P.

The four carry the annotation “Uncharacterized lineage-specific protein” and an unnumbered family symbol, because no publication assigns them a function distinct from their non-duplicated partners. That is the honest name, and it is also the rule: a paralogue with no literature function is not given one here.

Of 79 distinct annotation strings over 284 occurrences, exactly one controlled target can be reached by name: ORF1A, from polyprotein, 1a replicase and their variants. 72 strings remain unbound. The source calls both cassette copies minor glycoprotein — 174 times in Etaarterivirus and 156 in Zetaarterivirus — and a name that cannot distinguish a protein from its own paralogue cannot bin either one.

So the thirteen structural features were separated by position in the cassette, and each orthogroup got its own PSSM. Position curates; sequence calls. The collections are near-uniform in size as a result — GP2, GP2P, GP3P, GP4P, GP5, M and N all at 171, GP3 166, GP4 168, E 169, ORF5A 165 — because each is one member per genome rather than whatever the submitters happened to name.

EP is the exception at 23 members: the duplicated E is present in a subset of the lineages, and it is not padded out to match the others.

07What it cannot do

The limits, each measured rather than supposed.

Three genomes of 54 collapse part of the cassette onto one ORF, and the cause is downstream of the profile match rather than in it — the trace puts each feature in the right place, and the scan to a start and a stop then lands overlapping ORFs on the same coordinates. Recorded in detail below, and deliberately not patched.

The four paralogues are named “Uncharacterized lineage-specific protein” and that will not improve without new literature. No publication assigns GP2', GP3', GP4' or E' a function distinct from their partners. The module declines to invent one, and declines to make them essential.

The panel is 24 genomes. Every percentage on this page is over a set that small, and a single genome is four percentage points. The subfamily has 54 genomes in BV-BRC and 19 complete ones; no amount of care in the build makes that a large sample.

Use this module on complete simian arterivirus genomes, and use it rather than the Arteriviridae module whenever the cassette is duplicated — that is the whole reason it exists. The nine features carrying copy_num are called in every complete genome routed here, with no exceptions. The four paralogues are called at the same rate as their partners but carry no copy_num, because a protein with no published function must never be the thing that marks a genome defective.

08What the run found

What the build turned up that was not expected.

Three genomes of 54 collapse part of the cassette onto one ORF. In 2682610.4 (NC_048209) the layout is otherwise exactly right — GP2P 10806, GP3P 11354, GP4P 11878, GP2 12423, GP5 13732 — and the annotator's own trace puts GP3 at 12884-13210 and GP4 at 13229-13693, both correct. The collision happens after the profile match: ORF3 and ORF4 overlap in this family, and both features scan out to a start and a stop, so all three land on the same 271 aa ORF. 1885929.21 and .22 do the same with GP2 and GP2P. Recorded rather than patched.

Three nested calls are architecture, not defects. E inside GP2 (100% of calls), ORF1A inside ORF1AB (98%) and ORF5A inside GP5 (46%) are overlapping reading frames — ORF2b inside ORF2a, ORF5a inside ORF5. The default advice for a nested call is to raise the floor or drop the feature; here that would be exactly wrong, and the low similarity between the pairs (E vs GP2: 41% identity over 27% coverage, 19 bits) confirms they are different proteins sharing genome space.

Does the turnip yellow mosaic virus module actually work?

The cleanest layout in this build: three overlapping ORFs on one ~6.3 kb RNA, a ~1,830 aa replicase, a ~628 aa movement protein that overlaps it, and a ~189 aa coat protein from a subgenomic RNA.

01What the module covers

One genus, three ORFs, and a deliberate separation from its sibling.

Tymovirus and Marafivirus are both Tymoviridae, but a LowVan module is defined by genome organisation rather than phylogeny and the two layouts differ: tymoviruses carry a distinct overlapping movement protein and their coat protein is a separate 3'-proximal ORF, while marafiviruses carry CP inside ORF1 and have no equivalent MP. MP is bound before REP in the binning rules, because in tymoviruses the movement protein is defined by overlapping the replicase ORF — any source string naming both (overlapping protein/movement protein, overlapping out-of-phase protein) belongs to MP.

02How it was built

342 features, 96.2% binned, and three labels that carry no meaning at all.

TYMVgp1, TYMVgp2 and TYMVgp3 are positional labels — the U-number trap — and are not trusted as names. They are admitted only inside the three explicit length windows, and it is the window that actually decides them: gp1 is 628 aa (MP), gp2 is 1,844 (REP), gp3 is 189 (CP). A future record numbering its ORFs differently is rejected by the window rather than silently misfiled.

Length-encoded labels of the form unnamed protein product; 206K ORF (AA 1-1844) are handled the same way. The source also contains two outright misspellings of the same protein — RNA-dependend RNA polymerase and RNA-depedent RNA polymerase — which the rules absorb.

The reference-budget measurement predicted 92.2% (94 of 102) at a budget of 25, and 8 clusters holding 8 genomes were recorded as uncovered. In the run, 97 of 102 routed — three better than predicted, because BLASTn at annotation time is more permissive than the clustering used to choose the references. The budget figure is the conservative one and is the number to plan with.

Tymovirus is the only one of the four modules in this group that does not reach 100%. It is a small, diverse genus: 34 clusters over 102 genomes, so a long tail of singletons is expected and is a documented gap rather than a defect.

07What it cannot do

Two limits.

Five genomes do not route. 8 clusters holding 8 genomes were left uncovered by the 25-reference budget and 5 genomes failed to route in the run. They are the divergent tail of a small genus, and reaching them means either more references than the project budget allows or splitting a genus that is already small.

No mature peptides. The replicase is a polyprotein and its cleavage sites are not documented for this genus in a form that can be located from the sequence, so its products are not claimed. One record, Piper methysticum tymovirus 1 at 9,491 nt, is 40% longer than a normal tymovirus genome; it is classified as Tymovirus in BV-BRC and is kept, but it widens the segment length window.

08What the run found

Nothing broken, and one number better than predicted.

Routing came in three genomes above the budget estimate. That direction is worth noting because the opposite happened in Crinivirus, where a gate figure of 100% was an artefact of merging records by genome name and the honest number is 92.4%. A prediction that disagrees with the run is a finding either way; here the conservative estimate was simply conservative.

Nothing outstanding. Three of three features essential and called, 99.0% of the genus clean.

01What the module covers

One circular genome of about 8 kb, read in overlapping frames.

Gammapapillomavirus is the largest human cutaneous genus by type count, recovered mostly from skin swabs and from asymptomatic infection. The genome is a single circular double-stranded DNA molecule of roughly 7.3 kb. The module is built on genomes from humans, with a tail of primate types.

1 of the 7 core features are called on every routed exemplar. The lowest core rate is E6 at 98.0%.

The spliced features are called by a separate program on an exact donor/acceptor match, so their rate is a measurement of the junction and not of the profile: E1^E4 on 11.4%, E8^E2 on 5.8%.

The low rate is reported rather than tuned. Nothing was widened to raise it, because the same widening on Xipapillomavirus produced products the length of E2 rather than of E8^E2.

07What it cannot do

The limits, each measured rather than supposed.

The spliced features are the weakest in the family.

Every mature L2 call in this module begins immediately after a furin site in its own L2 precursor, which is the chemical invariant rather than a fixed offset: furin cleaves after R-X-[KR]-R. Across the family 648 of 681 calls on ambiguity-clean genomes cut after a full site (95.2%).

Use this module on complete Gammapapillomavirus genomes. It calls 7 core features on essentially every genome of the genus; the spliced features are called when the junction is present and readable, and their rate is stated above rather than tuned.

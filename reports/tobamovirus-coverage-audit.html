Does the tobacco mosaic virus module actually work?

Tobamovirus is the genus of tobacco mosaic virus — the first virus ever described, and the cause of tomato brown rugose fruit virus, which has closed greenhouses across four continents since 2014. This is the audit of the module built for it: what it covers, where its thresholds sit, whether genomes reach it, whether it calls the proteins, and what it cannot do. 01What the module covers

One genus, one genome organisation, one module — and for once that was not a judgement call.

Every tobamovirus carries the same four ORFs in the same order on one 6.4 kb positive-sense RNA: a 126K replication protein, a 183K read-through product that adds the polymerase domain, a movement protein, and a coat protein. There is no accessory gene that some species have and others lack, and no segment. Nothing here needed a second module.

The 126K and 183K proteins are not a cleaved precursor. Tobamoviruses encode no protease; the two are separate translation products of the same ORF, the longer one made when ribosomes read through an amber stop at roughly 69% of its length. The module's first annotation strings called both “Nonstructural polyprotein”, which was wrong twice over — wrong biologically, and a schema violation that cost the whole first quality run. See §08.

02How it was built

Standard pipeline, with one departure forced by the read-through.

Both replicase features were built with -x, which retains X residues. BV-BRC already encodes the read-through position as X in 565 of 613 REP183 sequences; without -x the pipeline discards any sequence carrying an ambiguous residue, which would have left REP183 with 48 sequences instead of 613.

The two replicase collections are separated by length, not by annotation string, because the source data uses the same words for both. REP126 takes 980–1199 aa and REP183 1400–1780. An earlier, looser window (1000–1399) spanned the valley between the two populations and let a 1,220-aa sequence seed a leftover profile that out-scored the genuine ones, mis-calling the 126K as 926 aa starting at nt 639 instead of 1,116 aa at nt 69.

REP183 sits higher than REP126 deliberately. The 183K contains the 126K as its first ~1,116 residues, so a REP126 profile scores well at the 183K locus and the two would otherwise compete. Length windows and the higher cutoff keep them apart; the cross-feature QC still reports the pair as near-identical, which is correct and expected rather than actionable — a read-through pair shares its N-terminus by construction.

Self-recall against each feature's own in-range collection: REP126 604/607 (99.5%), REP183 609/613 (99.3%), MP 460/461 (99.8%), CP 794/796 (99.7%). This is a floor, not a validation — profiles finding their own training data proves little. The held-out figures are in §05 and §06.

Of 102 near-complete exemplars, 99 route and 92 of those 99 get the complete set of four. Five get exactly one feature, one gets two, and one gets five because the 183K is called twice on a frameshifted genome (§07). No routed exemplar comes back empty.

The rates above are per routed genome, not per call: the 183K is called 94 times across 93 genomes, and counting calls rather than genomes would overstate it. The denominator is the 99 that routed, because a genome no rep contig claims is a routing failure and belongs in §04, not here.

This figure was published as 95.5% and that was wrong twice. Five genomes routed, cleared no profile, and were dropped before scoring, so the run reported 89 scored and 85/89 = 95.5%. The true denominator is 94. And run_gto_eval.py decides good-versus-poor on genome and contig flags only — feature-level flags are printed four lines lower and ignored — so one of the 85 carries a length flag and is not clean. 84 of 94 = 89.4%.

Two bugs, in series. annotate_by_viral_pssm.pl emits a no_features_called sentinel row so a routed genome does not lose its taxon assignment; the GTO wrapper pushed that row as a feature, and GenomeTypeObject died on its missing location. Fixing that only moved the failure, because viral_genome_quality.pl opens with $genome_in->{features}->[0] or die. A genome that called nothing is not unscoreable — it is the worst case, and the bucket exists for it. Both are fixed in the kit.

The other modules were checked rather than assumed. Hepeviridae is unaffected: all 47 of its unscored genomes are unrouted, with no viral_family field at all, so they fail a different and correct check and are already counted in its 622/669 routing figure. Its denominator of 622 is exactly the set that routed, which is the right one, and its 89.2% stands. Tobamovirus was inflated because its five had routed.

88 of 7,843 features (1.1%) matched no binning rule. Most are positional or mass labels the rules deliberately do not trust — “hypothetical protein” (8), “charged protein” (5), “unnamed protein product” (4). Thirteen of them, however, are canonical tobamovirus names that should have been caught: “30K protein” (5) and “29K protein” (5) are the movement protein, “17 kDa protein” (3) the coat protein. Adding mass-based rules for those would recover thirteen sequences out of 7,843. It has not been done, and this line is the record that it is outstanding.

Five genomes route and call nothing at all — 5.3% of the panel, and the single largest gap in the module. They are not plant tobamoviruses:

2052571.3 Podosphaera prunicola tobamo-like virus, 11,449 nt
2743167.3 Erysiphe necator associated tobamo-like virus, 11,430 nt
2501286.3 Acidomyces richmondensis tobamo-like virus, 10,291 nt
2859595.4 Rubber tree latent virus 1, 11,923 nt
2950833.3 Plant associated tobamo-like virus 1, 6,553 nt

Three are viruses of powdery-mildew and acidophilic fungi, and four have genomes roughly twice the length of a tobamovirus. They reach the module because BLASTn against the rep contigs is more permissive than the protein profiles are — 2950833.3 routes at 10,241 bits and then clears no profile at any feature. Widening the profiles to cover them would not be fixing a threshold; it would be admitting a different group of viruses into a genus module. The honest options are to leave them uncalled, as here, or to tighten routing so the module stops claiming them.

No mature peptides, and that is correct. There is no protease in the tobamovirus genome and nothing in this module is cleaved, so there is no _MAT or _SP feature to miss.

The module cannot call a replicase below roughly 40% identity to its profiles, and does not attempt to stitch a frameshifted genome back together. Three of the four flagged genomes fail for the second reason. Neither is a threshold that can be tuned — lowering the cutoff on a 36%-identity target would admit far more noise than signal.

08What the run found

The module shipped twice with a defect the earlier steps could not see.

first quality run
  good (no flags)           0    0.0%
  poor (flagged)           89  100.0%

  87  Genome has too many HSPs for: Nonstructural polyprotein; Count = 2
  52  Feature is too short
  38  Feature is too long

REP126 and REP183 had both been declared with the annotation string Nonstructural polyprotein. viral_genome_quality.pl keys its %essential hash by that string rather than by the feature key, so the second feature read silently overwrote the first one's length window, and %anno_count summed both features' calls against one copy_num. The script runs once per genome and Perl randomises hash order, so a different window won each time — 52 genomes reading “too short”, 38 “too long”, one flag per genome. Nothing was wrong with the profiles, the cutoffs or the genomes.

install_module.py --check, check_annotations.py and qc_cross_feature.py all passed. It surfaced only at the quality run, and it surfaced disguised: the skill's own rule of thumb says “‘Feature is too short’ dominating means the length bounds are wrong”, and here both flags fire and neither is about the bounds. check_annotations.py now fails on the collision.

Fixing it exposed a second, genuine annotator bug. Odontoglossum ringspot virus has its amber at residue 1112 and its real terminator at 1596, against a 1,616-residue profile, so the alignment overran the terminator and the HSP held two stops. internal_stop: 1 is meant to mean “read through one stop and end at the next”; instead the code called the match broken and cropped the gene at the first stop, returning the 126K-length product under the 183K name. It now terminates at the stop after the read-through, gated on the feature's min_len so that a genome riddled with frameshift stops does not get a fragment instead. ORSV calls 1,596 aa.

0.0% → 95.5% good. The whole difference was two annotation strings and one gated line in the annotator; not a single profile changed.

Tobamovirus submitters spell the 126K replication protein 108 different ways and the 183K 102 — “small replicase subunit”, “129K protein”, “replication-associated protein”, “126 kDa replicase”. Eighty-eight records call the 126K protein “RNA-dependent RNA polymerase”, which it is not: the polymerase domain exists only in the read-through product. That single confusion is the clearest argument in this module for a controlled vocabulary.

Rarefaction over 100 shuffles: the free-text vocabulary reaches 118 distinct strings and is still climbing at the end of the set, the controlled vocabulary saturates at 4 by the 25th genome. 121.6 strings per 100 genomes against 4.1 — a 29.5-fold collapse, the strongest of the modules built so far (Hepeviridae 15.8×, Matonaviridae 4.3×).

One new vocabulary string added (“Methyltransferase and helicase replication protein”); the other three reuse strings the controlled set already had. The collapse figure here was first published as 55.8× and is 66.8×. collect_synmap.py was counting the 49 unbound strings as if they bound to a fifth feature called UNASSIGNED, so the page simultaneously claimed “0 unbound” and divided by one target too many. It also omitted the homology-rescued records entirely. Both fixed; the unbound 49 are now listed on the collapse page instead of hidden inside it.

Does the single-movement-protein module actually work?

Betaflexiviridae_MP is the half of the family that moves by one movement protein rather than a triple gene block — Trichovirus, Vitivirus, Citrivirus, Tepovirus, Prunevirus, Chordovirus, Divavirus, Wamavirus. Between them: apple chlorotic leaf spot, grapevine Pinot gris disease, citrus leaf blotch, cherry mottle leaf. This is the audit of the module built for them. 01What the module covers

Half a family, and the half was chosen by gene layout rather than phylogeny.

Betaflexiviridae splits cleanly in two on how its members move between cells. One half carries a triple gene block; this one carries a single movement protein. Splitting on that is not a tidiness exercise — it is worth 16 percentage points of routing: the family lumped reaches 75.3% of its genomes on 25 references, this half reaches 91.6% and the TGB half 81.0%.

Capillovirus is deliberately absent. Its coat protein sits inside the ORF1 polyprotein — residues 1909-2100 of 2105, measured by BLASTp against Trichovirus and Tepovirus CPs — so it has no separate CP coding sequence and cannot share this module's CP feature. It gets its own module.

The replicase is one uncleaved ORF carrying methyltransferase, helicase and polymerase domains. Betaflexiviridae encodes no protease, so “polyprotein” would be wrong here for the same reason it is wrong for the tobamovirus replicase, and the annotation names the domain that distinguishes it.

VITI_ORF2 mostly has no name to bin on. 235 of its 289 source records say only “hypothetical protein” and the rest give a mass. It is nonetheless one homology group — 426 Vitivirus sequences, 67 of 70 sampled hitting another member at a median 97% identity, and not one hit to CP or NABP — so it reaches the collection through the homology rescue rather than through a text rule, and ships with the uncharacterized annotation because no paper establishes a function for it.

A genus-specific accessory must not fire on a sibling genus, so its cutoff is set between two measurements rather than from one. Across 200 sampled genomes, NABP scores at most 39 bits in the genera that lack it (Tepovirus 39, Trichovirus 38, Prunevirus 38) against a weakest genuine Vitivirus signal of 75; 60 sits between them. VITI_ORF2 produced no hit at all outside Vitivirus, so there is no noise to exclude and it is set low for sensitivity.

In-range self-recall: REP 1204/1204 (100%), CP 1790/1791 (99.9%), MP 1296/1297 (99.9%), VITI_ORF2 288/289 (99.7%), NABP 463/470 (98.5%). A floor, not a validation — profiles finding their own training data proves little. The held-out figures are in §05 and §06.

The accessory rates are the taxonomy, not the module. Quoting NABP as 45.7% and VITI_ORF2 as 37.8% of the module would describe how much of the module is Vitivirus. Per genus: NABP is called on 169 of 183 Vitivirus (92%) and 3 of 8 Prunevirus, and on none of Citrivirus, Chordovirus, Divavirus, Tepovirus or Wamavirus. VITI_ORF2 is called on 154 of 183 Vitivirus (84%) and on nothing else anywhere.

NABP also fires on 14 Trichovirus genomes, and that is real. All 14 are Cherry mottle leaf virus, Peach mosaic virus or Mexico trichovirus, all 7,972-8,018 nt against Trichovirus mali at 7,328-7,561 — those species carry an extra ORF. It is a species-level architecture difference inside the genus, comfortably above the 39-bit noise ceiling, and not a false positive.

No routed exemplar comes back empty, and 377 of 407 carry three or more features.

85 of 96, not 89. run_gto_eval.py decides good-versus-poor on genome and contig flags only; feature-level flags are printed four lines lower and do not count. 553 of 11,260 features (4.9%) matched no binning rule, across 42 distinct strings after the rescue recovered 351. The residue is genuinely uninformative — “hypothetical protein”, “unknown”, bare ORF numbers — and is listed in full on the collapse page rather than hidden.

The module cannot call a replicase that is materially shorter than its profiles expect, and does not reassemble a reading frame broken by ambiguous bases. Those two account for six of the seven flagged genomes. Neither is a threshold that tuning would fix.

08What the run found

Two profile defects, and every automated check passed both.

One bad profile suppressed the coat protein on 17% of a genus. CP was uncalled on 26 genomes, all Trichovirus mali, at full contig length. Not a threshold:

Nucleocapsid protein  6616-7362  451 bits
Match contains 1 stop codon(s), cropping
Coverage prior to stop codon (0.072) is lower than cutoff: 0.65

That cluster held 11 sequences of 249-250 aa against a modal CP of 193, all annotated from the same upstream Met; the correct start is the Met at residue 58. Its profile therefore anchored 57 codons early, landed inside the movement protein ORF in another frame, hit a stop, and was rejected — while scoring 451 bits and beating the clean profiles at 263 and 247 that would have called the protein. The pipeline's N-terminal check logged it OK, because that check asks whether a cluster's members agree with each other and all 11 agreed perfectly. Trimmed to the right Met, 26 of 26 recover.

A length window set from the dominant genera deleted a genus. CP was built at 150-280 because Vitivirus and Trichovirus sit at 193-198 aa. Citrus leaf blotch virus coat protein is ~41 kDa — median 363 — so 90 real Citrivirus CPs went to the outliers file and the collection kept 5 atypical short ones. The profiles were then built with almost no Citrivirus, and the quality run flagged Citrivirus CP “too long” against a max_len computed from that same truncated collection. Widening to 150-400 takes Citrivirus CP from 5 sequences to 95.

CP 92.1% → 98.5%, Trichovirus CP 83% → 100%, no other feature moved. Predicted from retesting the 26 failures and then confirmed by a full 409-exemplar re-run, which returned exactly the predicted figure.

Rarefaction over 100 shuffles: free text climbs to 86 distinct strings and is still rising at the end of the set, the controlled vocabulary saturates at 5 by the fifth genome — 21.5 strings per 100 genomes against 1.2, a 17.2-fold difference.

The sharpest case is VITI_ORF2: 246 of its records say nothing but “hypothetical protein” and were placed by homology, not by their text. A generic string becoming a specific annotation is the strongest form this collapse takes, and it is invisible to any count that reads only the binning rules.

One new vocabulary string (“Nucleic acid-binding protein”); the other four reuse strings the controlled set already had. The gene symbol Viti_ORF2 carries a lineage prefix because a bare ORF2 already names a different protein in Hepeviridae.

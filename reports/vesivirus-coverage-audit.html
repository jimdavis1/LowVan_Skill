LowVan · Vesivirus module · 14 October 2026

Vesivirus

Feline calicivirus and its relatives. Three open reading frames, as in Norovirus, but with something no other calicivirus genus has: ORF2 is itself a precursor, cleaved to release a leader protein and the mature capsid. That makes eleven features, the most in the family, and one of them is the only feature in any LowVan module derived by cutting its parent collection — because the mature capsid is deposited by nobody. This audit says what the module calls, what it refuses, and which of the remaining gaps are biology and which are ours.

01What the module covers

One genus, one module, and a genome organisation that is unique in the family at one point.

Feline calicivirus causes upper respiratory and oral disease in cats and is the genus's type member and the best-studied calicivirus of any genus, being one of the few that grows in culture. The rest are animal viruses: San Miguel sea lion virus, vesicular exanthema of swine virus, and canine, mink, rabbit, bovine, walrus, reptile and primate vesiviruses.

ORF1  p5.6 - p32 - p39 (NTPase) - p30 - VPg - Pro-Pol      1763 aa
ORF2  LC + VP1                                              668 aa
ORF3  VP2                                                   106 aa

Three ORFs puts this genus with Norovirus rather than Sapovirus or Lagovirus, where the capsid is in frame with the polyprotein. But ORF2 is a precursor: the viral protease cuts it to release a 124-residue leader and the 544-residue mature capsid, which no other calicivirus genus does. Pro and Pol stay joined as a single product, as in Sapovirus and unlike Lagovirus.

02How it was built

The vocabulary reproduced the whole FCV cleavage map, and then corrected two errors in this project's own notes.

The FCV map (PMC136307) gives five essential ORF1 sites plus the ORF2 leader cut. FCV is the genus where P1′ is most variable — D, N, S and A all appear — so a P1′ whitelist drawn from norovirus alone would reject real sites here.

The modal deposited length of the records carrying each product name is the literature value in every single case:

Two corrections to this project's own cleavage notes came out of that measurement. The ORF2 site was recorded as E157/S158, which contradicted the 14.2 kDa leader described in the same line — 14.2 kDa is about 124 residues, not 157. And P1′ is A in 275 of 323 precursors, not S in 39. The cut is E124/A125. Both were corrected in CLEAVAGE_SITES.md with their evidence rather than silently overwritten.

The mature capsid had to be cut, because nothing deposits it. Every record annotated VP1 in this genus is the 668-residue precursor. So VP1 is derived from the precursor collection, the way rotavirus VP8*/VP5* are derived from VP4 — and as in that case the site is a motif inside a window, never a column number, so it re-locates itself after any re-clustering.

[FIL]R[LIV]E(?=[ASG])    present 95.0%   unique 95.0%   cuts at 124 in 94.4%

446 precursors cut -> VP1 median 544 (literature 544), LC remainder 124 (literature 124)

One string was refused rather than bound: p30-VPg, 206 records at a median of 386 residues, which is p30 (275) plus VPg (111). It is a processing intermediate, not a mature product, and binding it to either feature would have trained that profile on a protein half again its length.

Essentiality is set from measured call rates, not from intuition. The four features called on 97–99% of routed genomes carry copy_num; the six ORF1 products and VP1, at 76–78%, do not. §07 says why that split is where it is, and it is the same reasoning that made rotavirus VP8*/VP5* non-essential.

04Does it route?

Everything routes.

276 of 276 exemplars routed, 0 unrouted. The budget measurement over all 382 genomes ≥ 6 kb gives 100% at 19 references, inside the project's budget of 25. Routing is not a limitation in this module.

ORF1, over 210 complete genomes: sum(products) − polyprotein = −1 aa, with p05 and p95 both −1. Zero spread, and all five junctions have zero overlaps. ORF2, over 212: sum(products) − precursor = 0, and LC/VP1 overlaps on 0 of 212. Six of the eight products land exactly on their literature lengths and two within one residue.
The derived VP1 is the tightest capsid in the family — median 545 against a literature 544, p05 543, p95 546. Cutting a parent collection at a measured motif produced a better feature than building from deposits would have, because the deposits do not contain this protein at all.

NS1 is worth a line of its own. At 46 residues it is the shortest feature in any calicivirus module, and short features have been the hard ones — sapovirus NS1 at 69 residues could not be called well at any cutoff without being placed wrongly. This one is called at a median of exactly 46 with p05 and p95 both 46. Vesivirus p5.6 is simply invariant.

Self-recall against each feature's own in-range collection: 100.0% on all eleven features. A floor rather than a validation.
Held-out panel, 40 genomes stratified across ten host groups — FCV, SMSV, VESV, canine, mink, rabbit, bovine, sea lion, walrus and primate: 39 of 40 clean. The one exception is a bovine calicivirus on which nothing was called at all; it is the same gap as §07.
Cross-feature contamination audit: 2,961 sequences against 32 cluster references, nothing above 50 bits. That matters more here than in most modules, because VP1 and LC are both subsequences of CAPSID.

07What it cannot do

One gap, and it is a clean split rather than a scatter.

The six ORF1 products and VP1 are feline calicivirus features. Across the 268 genomes carrying a polyprotein: 210 of 212 FCV genomes have all six ORF1 products, and none of the 58 non-FCV genomes has any.

The cause is the vocabulary, not the biology. p5.6, p32, p39, p30 and Pro-Pol are FCV nomenclature, so the collections behind those features are FCV sequences, and the profiles do not reach a canine or mink vesivirus. The precursor is still called on those genomes — POLY at 97%, CAPSID at 97%, LC at 99%, VP2 at 97% — so a non-FCV vesivirus is annotated, just not dissected.

All seven are declared non-essential, so the genome-quality tool does not flag a canine vesivirus poor for lacking features that were never built from anything like it. The precedent is exact: rotavirus VP8* and VP5* are species-A features declared the same way, because their cleavage site is documented for species A and the identity to the other species is 19–33%.

Closing it needs collections for the non-FCV products, which means either finding their deposits under different names or deriving them from the polyprotein by motif, as VP1 was. It does not close by lowering a cutoff: self-recall is already 100% on every feature, so the profiles are not failing to find what they were built from — they were built from the wrong half of the genus.

08What the run found

One defect, in the measurement rather than the module, and it had been documented years before it was committed.

Self-recall was being measured with the wrong search. collect_registry.py ran psiblast without -comp_based_stats 0, so it used the default. Composition-based statistics rescale a score by the subject's amino-acid composition, and that costs a short profile far more than a long one.

                      CBS 2 (as measured)    CBS 0 (correct)
NS1   46 residues          105/158  66.5%       158/158  100%
NS5  111 residues           66/184  35.9%       184/184  100%
every other feature           100%               100%

Every bit_cutoff in every module JSON was chosen against scores measured at -comp_based_stats 0, so measuring recall at the default compared a threshold to a different scale. The error was always present and only became visible on the module with the shortest features; the three Caliciviridae modules shipped before this one reported 99.99%, 100% and 99.9%, high enough to hide it. Re-measured correctly, all three read 100.0% on every feature.

This project had already written the trap down. references/curation.md records “same PSSM, same subjects: -comp_based_stats 2 → median 22 bits, -comp_based_stats 0 → median 43 bits” and the instruction “measure with the search you will actually use” — and the annotator does not even use psiblast, it runs tblastn against the genome, where the same profiles score higher again. The documentation was right and the tool did not follow it.

09What the vocabulary bought

The point of the module, stated as a ratio.

96 distinct annotation strings across 8,579 occurrences collapse onto 11 controlled annotations — an 8.7× reduction, with 21 strings left unbound.

Rarefied over proteins rather than genomes, and compared at the depth both curves reach, 2,477 proteins: the source vocabulary holds 58.7 distinct strings against the module's 12.0, a 4.9× collapse.

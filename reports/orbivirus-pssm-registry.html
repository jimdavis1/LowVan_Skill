One module for the whole genus, and it was measured rather than assumed. All eighteen virus groups in this dump — Culicoides-borne (bluetongue, African horse sickness, epizootic haemorrhagic disease, equine encephalosis, Palyam) and tick-borne (Kemerovo, Tribec, Great Island, Wad Medani, St Croix River) alike — carry the same ten segments encoding VP1–VP7 and NS1–NS3. Bluetongue virus is 64% of the dump, which is why every length window here is explicit rather than taken from a pooled median.

What was missed, and why

Three features fall short of the 85% threshold that earns copy_num, and none of them is a profile problem. NS5 (54.6%) is a bluetongue protein that other orbiviruses largely do not encode. NS4 (78.9%) is lineage-patchy and three-fold variable in length. VP2 (77.5% on the group-weighted panel, 100% on the BTV-dominated coverage exemplars) is the homology split described above. The gap between those two VP2 numbers is itself the finding: a panel drawn in proportion to the dump would have been almost all bluetongue and would have reported VP2 at 100% while testing nothing.

There are no derived features in this module — no cleaved precursor, no mature peptide. What there is instead is a feature that is not one homology group. VP2 is called in every Culicoides-borne orbivirus and in none of the tick-borne ones. All nine panel genomes lacking a VP2 call are Kemerovo, Tribec, St Croix River, Wad Medani or Umatilla, and their VP2 records align to the bluetongue canonical at 24–33% identity over 33–88 residues, bitscores of 17–36 — which is to say not at all. VP2 is a positional label that spans two protein families, exactly the trap the U-number rule warns about, and it is why VP2 carries no copy_num.

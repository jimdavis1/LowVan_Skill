LowVan · Kolmioviridae module · 27 November 2026

A 1.7 kb genome with one gene on it was the clue

This module shipped in November 2026 calling a single protein. On the genome map, half the genome sat empty. That is not what viral genomes look like, and it was the right thing to notice. The gap was ORF2: a second gene on the opposite strand, read by ambisense transcription, which had been refused on the strength of one negative experiment in one species. It is now built, from 36 of the module's 41 genomes, and none of its sequences came from a database — no submitter has ever annotated one.

01The gene that was refused, and why the refusal was wrong

One negative experiment in one species is not a claim about a family.

The first build of this module declared ORF2 known-absent across the whole family. The evidence was one experiment: in Swiss snake colony virus 1 the ORF2 initiation codon was mutated to isoleucine, and replication, persistence and particle formation were all unaffected, with no ORF2 mRNA detectable (PMID 42842300). That result is sound, and it is about one virus in boa constrictor cells.

A refusal is a claim about every genome in the module, so it needs the search for the positive as much as for the negative. That search was never run. It finds Kishimoto and colleagues (bioRxiv 2025.01.08.631871 v3), who show ORF2 in Serinus canaria kolmiovirus is expressed by ambisense transcription, yields two isoforms by alternative splicing, and promotes helper-dependent transmission with avian bornavirus helpers. They report genomic-sense ORFs with poly-A signals in five further kolmiovirus lineages.

The same paper supplies the architecture that makes ORF2 findable: it lies on the genomic-sense strand in the region complementary to the antigen gene. The snake paper states it independently — ‘two partially overlapping >500 base ORFs, the DAg ORF in the antigenomic orientation and the ORF2 in the genomic orientation’. The first build had used the opposite rule, treating an ORF antisense to the antigen as a shadow and discarding it. That is why nothing was found.

02Finding a protein no database contains

Zero of 3,368 deposited sequences are an ORF2. Every member was translated from a genome.

Zero of the 3,368 unique protein sequences in the BV-BRC dump for this family are an ORF2. Not rare — absent. It appears in no annotation string, so it can never surface as an unassigned bin, a low count or a gap; every member of this collection was translated from the module's own genomes.

The rule: locate the antigen in each genome by its own profiles, call every ORF on the opposite strand circularly and at 60 aa or more, and take the longest whose span is at least 40% complementary to the antigen gene. Thirty-six of the 41 genomes carry one, 65 to 212 aa, median 149 — which is exactly the length of the spliced canary ORF2b reported in the preprint's Supplementary Table S3.

Homology then corrected the rule in three genomes, which is the order it should happen in. In Desmodus rotundus deltavirus-B CAJ1 the positional rule picked a 120 aa ORF and the profiles called a 73 aa one instead — the orthologue of the ORF in both sibling DrDV-B genomes. In two rodent deltaviruses the ORF2 start codon falls inside a run of Ns, so no ATG was there to find; both genomes are already flagged for ambiguous bases, and both were dropped from training while the locus is still called.

03Is ORF2 one gene or eleven?

A shared position, not a shared ancestry. Seventeen profiles say so.

Eleven, sharing a position. Mask the part of each ORF2 that lies antisense to the antigen and blast what is left against itself: matches survive only within a genus — snake against snake at 100%, the three bird agents at 97–100%, and across genera no more than snake against marmot, 45% identity over 22 residues at e=0.002. The cross-lineage similarity in the unmasked protein is the antigen's own antisense signal, not ORF2's.

Both source papers say the same thing in words — the product shows no substantial similarity to any known protein and no conserved domain. So the feature is annotated Uncharacterized lineage-specific ORF2 protein, carries 17 profiles rather than one, 12 of them built from a single sequence, and takes no copy_num: uncharacterized, lineage-specific, and absent from five genomes, any one of which would be sufficient on its own.

What the module does not claim is that these are one protein family. A shared genome position is what makes them one feature; it is not evidence of common descent, and the page says so rather than letting 17 profiles under one name imply it.

The antisense nesting is declared architecture rather than a duplicate call, and it was measured before it was declared: every one of the 36 ORF2 calls sits on the strand opposite the antigen, and in half of them it lies wholly inside the antigen gene. That is the arrangement both papers describe, and a call on the same strand would have been a defect.

Thirteen of these genomes are still filed by BV-BRC under genus Deltavirus — termite agents, bat and rodent deltaviruses, the fish and avian HDV-like agents, Epsilon virus. ICTV moved them. Both the scores and the routing agree with ICTV, so the module follows the sequence and not the metadata column.

05Why this is its own module, and where the ORF2 cutoff comes from

The threshold is set by the other module, not by this one.

DAg and the hepatitis delta virus S-HDAg are the same protein, in different lineages, on the same locus. Inside one module the annotator called both on 601 of 660 genomes. No bit cutoff fixes it — DAg scores 35–442 bits on hepatitis delta virus against 157–432 on its own genomes, fully interleaved. Rep-contig routing separates them 659 of 659, so the family is partitioned by reference set.

ORF2 inherits that problem in a new form, and this is the measurement that set its threshold. Because ORF2 lies complementary to the antigen, its profiles carry the antigen's antisense signal — and scored directly against 60 hepatitis delta virus genomes, six-frame translated and circularly doubled, they reach 86.6 bits on HDV's own antigen locus.

Routing already keeps hepatitis delta virus away from these profiles — 40 HDV genomes run end to end routed to Deltavirus 40 times out of 40 and produced no ORF2 call at all — but the cutoff no longer depends on routing holding.

06What it still cannot do

What the measurements could not reach, named rather than left out.

The spliced isoform is not built. Canary kolmiovirus makes two ORF2 proteins by alternative splicing, ORF2a at 135 aa unspliced and ORF2b at 149–150 aa spliced, across three genotypes. The donor and acceptor positions appear only in figures of the preprint, which the site rate-limited past reading. A splice feature built from anything other than published junctions is not permitted here, so only the unspliced form exists in this module.

The demonstrating genome is not in the module. BV-BRC holds the canary virus only as a 761 nt partial, below the length filter. The 16 complete genomes the preprint reports are DDBJ accessions BR002460–BR002472, and they are not yet released — NCBI, ENA and DDBJ all return nothing for them. So the one virus where ORF2 is experimentally proven contributes no sequence to the profiles that call it.

Five genomes have no ORF2 and that is an absence, not a threshold. Both Chinese fire belly newt virus 1 deposits, both fish agents and one of the two Epsilon virus deposits carry no genomic-sense ORF complementary to the antigen. No ORF2 profile scores above zero on any of them. Lowering the cutoff would not recover them because there is nothing there to recover.

Sampling is the standing limit. Ten genera are represented by one or two deposits each, 25 of the 33 profiles are built from a single sequence, and a profile built from one sequence can only be as general as that sequence. The vocabulary saturation page is in declined form for the same reason: 28 proteins are annotated by both BV-BRC and this module, below the floor of 40, so the raw collapse is reported instead of a curve drawn from too little data.

Every citation here is model-proposed. PMIDs 42842300 and 31321078 sit in PMID_claude_generated and no curator has confirmed them. The preprint has no PMID at all and is cited by DOI.

Does the cucumber mosaic virus module actually work?

Bromoviridae is a tripartite plant-virus family built at family level because all five genera share the layout: RNA1 carries 1a, RNA2 the RdRp and in the cucumoviruses 2b, RNA3 the movement protein and the coat protein. Cucumber mosaic virus, brome mosaic virus, alfalfa mosaic virus, tobacco streak virus. It routes 74.2% of complete genomes, and the remaining 25.8% receive no annotation at all — that is an accepted limit, closed on 30 September 2026, not outstanding work.

02How it was built

The naming trap: the source calls two different proteins “replicase”.

1a and 2a are different proteins on different segments, and the source calls both replicase. That string is therefore not binned on the word at all — it is routed to REP1 or REP2 by length against the two measured medians, and anything far below either is left unbinned rather than assigned to whichever is nearer.

§08 is about what happened when that routing was given no margin.

03Where the cutoffs sit

400 for 1a, 300 for the RdRp, 80 for the structural proteins.

REP2's max_len was 993 aa and is now 900. The old value was exactly REP1's median length, which is the whole of §08 in one number.

Cucumovirus is the natural boundary because it is the one real organisational difference in the family: it carries the 2b silencing suppressor on RNA2 and the other four genera do not, which is why P2B is called on 50.0% of routed genomes and correctly carries no copy_num.

07What it cannot do

Routing is the binding limit.

One genome in four of this family is not annotated by this module. 155 of 600 complete genomes match none of the 25 references, so the annotator never reaches them and they carry no features at all. Everything in §05 and §06 — the call rates, the 89.2% clean — describes only the 445 that did route. Read the two together: a high quality score on a partial denominator is not a high quality score on the family.

No mature peptides. Neither 1a nor 2a has cleavage sites documented for this family in a form that can be located from the sequence.

The evaluation is a sample — 148 genomes at ≥6 kb, seed 29. Routing in §04 is a census.

The homology rescue has now run on this module. Completed 1 October 2026, closing the item deferred on 30 September. rescue_unassigned.py takes every protein no annotation-string rule matched, blastp’s it against this module’s own collections and adopts each into the feature it matches at ≥80% identity over ≥60% of the query, refusing everything below that rather than sweeping it into a grab-bag. It had never run on this module because it crashed on the trailing tab BV-BRC writes into uniq.seq. 816 of 1253 unassigned sequences were adopted; the training set went 2904 → 3568 sequences (+23%) and the profile set 124 → 135. Exactly neutral, and only after a correction. Four 993-aa sequences annotated RNA-dependent RNA polymerase entered REP2; blastp puts them at 97–99.6% identity to REP1 across all 993 residues, so they are 1a proteins mislabelled at source. They raised REP2’s max_len from 886 to 993 and produced a profile that called the 1a ORF a second time, flagging Too many contigs for RNA2 on 8 genomes. Removed to REP2.rejected_1a.fasta. The check that catches this: REP2 held no sequence above 900 aa before the rescue and four after — a collection whose maximum length jumps is a binning change, not a coverage gain. Measured against a control — the shipped module scored on the same 139 genomes with the current JSON — the rescue moved genuinely-clean from 84.9% to 84.9% (0 genome(s) improved, 0 regressed). The earlier comparison against measurements/quality.tsv was discarded: that table predates the 30 September copy_num and vocabulary edits, which on some modules dominate the rescue entirely.

08What the run found

Two profiles calling one gene, on a third of all genomes.

REP1 and REP2 were called on identical coordinates in 32% of genomes. They are 1a and 2a — different proteins on different segments — so sharing coordinates is not merely suspicious, it is impossible.

REP2 collection   n=590   median 840 aa   max 993 aa
REP1 collection   n=293   median 993 aa   min 908 aa

3 of REP2's 590 members hit REP1 at 97% identity / 100% coverage
REP2 max_len was 993 -- exactly REP1's median length

Three 1a sequences had been binned into the REP2 collection. They formed their own cluster, produced their own profile, and that profile fired on 1a — and REP2's length window, whose ceiling was exactly REP1's median, admitted the call. The module's own build notes warn that the source calls both proteins “replicase”; the length routing that was supposed to separate them had no margin at the top.

Removing the three sequences, rebuilding REP2 (587 sequences, 29 profiles) and tightening max_len to 900 took the module from 43.9% to 89.2% genuinely clean and eliminated the duplicate calls entirely.

Fixed. Three contaminating sequences and one length ceiling, worth 45 points of genome quality.

The routing gap was accepted rather than fixed. The three-way split that would take the family from 74.2% to roughly 90% is measured, documented and undone. That was a judgement about where effort goes, made with these numbers in hand, and it applies to this family only.

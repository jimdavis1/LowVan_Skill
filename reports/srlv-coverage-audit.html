01What the module calls

Five features, and a tat that is collinear rather than spliced.

SRLV tat is a single-exon protein, also published as S, and it was MEASURED collinear by tblastn of each deposited protein against its own genome rather than assumed spliced from the HIV-1 layout. It therefore gets a PSSM and a copy_num like any other feature. EIAV tat, in the sister module, is genuinely spliced and gets neither.

02Where it does not call, and why

No declared feature lacks a profile. rev appears in the vocabulary and bins to its own key, but the module does not declare it: it is spliced, and nothing here calls spliced features.

04Vocabulary saturation

How fast each vocabulary stops growing as genomes are added.

The deposited vocabulary keeps growing; the module's does not. At the depth both reach, 34.3 distinct source strings correspond to 5.0 controlled annotations, a 6.9-fold collapse. The module reaches 95% of its final vocabulary after 14 proteins against the source's 251.

05Method

Coverage is measured by annotating de-replicated exemplars through the real GTO pipeline — annotate_by_viral_pssm-GTO.pl, then the transcript-edit and splice passes, then viral_genome_quality.pl — not by a reimplementation. Spliced features are included, which the flat feature-table path cannot represent. Rates are per exemplar, and exemplars are clustered at 95% nucleotide identity, so one row is one distinct sequence rather than one deposit.

Measured 27 October 2026 from the shipped module: the SRLV block of Viral_PSSM.json, its profiles and collections, and the 27 October coverage re-annotation over 51 exemplars de-replicated from 265 genomes. Vocabulary figures come from collections/synonyms.tsv, written by the module's own builder from the naming rules, and count deposited features rather than unique sequences.

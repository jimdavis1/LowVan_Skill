A controlled vocabulary stops growing. Free text does not.

Every rubivirus encodes the same seven proteins, so a curated vocabulary should need exactly seven names no matter how many genomes you read. Free-text product strings have no such ceiling: each new submitter spells the same proteins a new way. Sampling 400 annotated genomes 100 times over shows the two curves doing exactly that — the module is saturated after one genome, while the source vocabulary is still climbing at 400.

5strings, controlled vocabulary
86strings, BV-BRC free text

360genomes before free text saturates

The ratio is the number worth quoting: at the end of each curve, free text costs 13.9 strings per 100 genomes and the controlled vocabulary 3.2 — a 4.3-fold collapse. Reaching 95% of its final size takes the source vocabulary 360 genomes; the module gets there on the first. Across the whole dump, 58 distinct BV-BRC strings collapse into these 7 over 6,547 protein occurrences.

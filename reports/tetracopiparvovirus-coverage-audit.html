01What the module covers

Two genera held in one module because their profiles do not compete.

The module covers Tetraparvovirus and Copiparvovirus. They share a genome layout, their peripheral profiles do not cross-match, and neither brings a special feature that the other would contest — which is the test for whether a set of genera is one module or several.

Four features are declared: NS1, the capsid pair VP1 and VP2, and ORF3. NS1 and VP1 carry copy_num.

ORF3 is named uncharacterized lineage specific. Sixteen sequences cluster, they are callable, and what the protein does is not established in the literature — so the module names the slot and not a function.

07What it cannot do

Four limits, each measured rather than supposed.

Eight contigs are too long for the declared geometry. That is a statement about the deposits, not the module: a 300-genome panel of a genus whose members are routinely sequenced as part of larger assemblies will contain entries that are not single parvovirus genomes.

VP1's length window spans 560 to 1,261 residues. One feature covering two genera with different capsid sizes means the window cannot discriminate much, and a too-short or too-long flag on VP1 here carries less information than the same flag in a single-genus module.

Fourteen sequences are unassigned, most of them filed as putative. They are tracked in UNASSIGNED_TRACKING.tsv, which is a list of unfinished work, not a resolution. These are proteins the module does call, under deposits it did not reach.

No splice features are declared, and nothing in the collections showed the two-exon signature that put NS2 and NS3 into the splice caller in Amdoparvovirus and Protoparvovirus.

Use this module for tetraparvovirus and copiparvovirus genomes; routing is clean and NS1 and VP1 are reliable. Treat VP1 length flags with suspicion — the window spans two genera — and check whether a flagged contig is a whole genome before blaming the call.

08What the run found

What the build turned up that was not expected.

The capsid labels were wrong on a large scale and only applying copy_num exposed it. Before the capsid rebuild, 117 of 300 genomes had VP1 and VP2 called at identical coordinates: the two profiles had been built from deposits that disagreed about which nested form they held, so both landed on the same open reading frame.

Rebinning the nested forms by size per species rather than by the name on the deposit, and rebuilding the profiles, took that from 117 of 300 to 0. The deposited name is a claim about which form a submitter thought they had; it is not a measurement.

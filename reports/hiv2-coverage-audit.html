HIV2 — HIV-2 and simian immunodeficiency virus

HIV-2 and the simian immunodeficiency viruses

HIV2 is a module name, not a taxon. It holds HIV-2 and the SIVs together, and it is majority SIV: 1,660 of its 2,113 collection sequences and 65 of its 110 coverage exemplars come from simian immunodeficiency virus, not from HIV-2. The module is separated from HIV-1 on layout rather than distance — the two differ by one accessory gene, vpx here against vpu there, which is the project's partitioning rule.

02Where it does not call, and why

VPR is called on 72.7% of exemplars and that is biology, not a gap. Thirty of the 110 genomes carry a single accessory gene in a 311 nt window where the rest carry two in 630 nt, and the BV-BRC reference annotation agrees those genomes have no vpr. VPR therefore carries no copy_num. The held-out panel read 95% because it is mostly HIV-2; the coverage set carries the full SIV spread.

04Vocabulary saturation

How fast each vocabulary stops growing as genomes are added.

The deposited vocabulary keeps growing; the module's does not. At the depth both reach, 44.6 distinct source strings correspond to 9.0 controlled annotations, a 5.0-fold collapse. The module reaches 95% of its final vocabulary after 27 proteins against the source's 778.

05Method

Coverage is measured by annotating de-replicated exemplars through the real GTO pipeline — annotate_by_viral_pssm-GTO.pl, then the transcript-edit and splice passes, then viral_genome_quality.pl — not by a reimplementation. Spliced features are included, which the flat feature-table path cannot represent. Rates are per exemplar, and exemplars are clustered at 95% nucleotide identity, so one row is one distinct sequence rather than one deposit.

Measured 27 October 2026 from the shipped module: the HIV2 block of Viral_PSSM.json, its profiles and collections, and the 27 October coverage re-annotation over 110 exemplars de-replicated from 693 genomes. Vocabulary figures come from collections/synonyms.tsv, written by the module's own builder from the naming rules, and count deposited features rather than unique sequences.

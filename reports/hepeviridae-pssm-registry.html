Annotation strings and the PSSMs that call them

Hepeviridae is the only family in class Alsuviricetes that infects humans: Paslahepevirus balayani is hepatitis E virus, around 20 million infections a year. It is also, conveniently, among the simplest genomes in the class — one RNA segment, three overlapping ORFs, no frameshift and no readthrough. The module declares four features and calls all four. The headline decision here is one the module deliberately does not make: ORF1 ships whole, because the evidence that it is cleaved does not exist.

Hepeviridae

4 of 4 features built · 63 PSSMs · 42% callable

One module for the whole family, and that was measured rather than assumed. Every near-complete genome in BV-BRC was bucketed by ORF architecture from its own CDS coordinates: Paslahepevirus 1226 of 1273 genomes with exactly three CDS, Avihepevirus 23 of 23, Chirohepevirus 12 of 13, Rocahepevirus 32 of 47 with four. Every genus is ORF1 + ORF2 capsid + ORF3, so a single module is right, with a fourth feature for the extra Rocahepevirus ORF. The apparent ORF2/ORF3 order swap between genomes is the two genes overlapping, not a difference in layout.

The family is dominated by one species to a degree that shapes everything downstream: 25,438 of 29,294 records are hepatitis E virus, and the median record in the family is 347 nucleotides. Only 1,574 records reach 6 kb. That is why the collections carry 8,464 length-outliers alongside 3,718 in-range sequences — the submissions are partial, not the rules wrong.

Why ORF1 is declared whole, and not cut into seven domains. RefSeq annotates seven domains on the 1,693 aa ORF1 of NC_001434 — methyltransferase, Y, papain-like protease, poly-proline hinge, X, helicase and RdRp. This module declares none of them as mature peptides, and that decision rests on the literature rather than on caution.

The seven-domain annotation descends from Koonin et al. 1992, PNAS (PMID 1518855), whose title is “Computer-assisted assignment of functional domains” and which describes them throughout as putative. It is a sequence-comparison prediction and was never a demonstration that ORF1 is cleaved.

The experimental record since then does not support cleavage. Perttilä, Spuul & Ahola 2013, J Gen Virol (PMID 23255617) expressed full-length pORF1 and six truncations in HeLa and Huh-7 cells under several vector systems, probing with antisera raised against the MT, HEL and POL domains, and concluded that “the weight of evidence supports the proposition that pORF1 is not subjected to specific proteolytic processing, which is unusual among animal positive-strand RNA viruses but common for plant viruses.” LeDesma et al. 2023, eLife (PMID 36852909) scanned the putative protease domain by mutagenesis and found that “ORF1 operates as a multifunctional protein, which is not subject to proteolytic processing” — the six essential cysteines coordinate divalent metal ions and hold the fold together, and the domain rescues replication only in the context of full-length ORF1, never as an isolated subdomain.

Declaring seven mature peptides would make the annotator assert seven cleavage events on every hepatitis E genome it touches. The domain boundaries are recorded in BUILD_NOTES.md §3 with their coordinates, so the decision can be revisited without re-deriving them. All three citations are model-proposed and carry the PMID_claude_generated flag; none has been confirmed by a curator.

What was missed, and why

Two real gaps, both measured and neither hidden.

ORF3 is not callable in Piscihepevirus or Chirohepevirus. The ORF3 collection holds 863 Paslahepevirus sequences against 2 Chirohepevirus and 0 Piscihepevirus. The single Piscihepevirus ORF3 is 225 aa, well outside the 70–160 aa window the other 974 sequences define. Raising or lowering a threshold cannot fix this; only more sequences can. Setting the ORF3 bit_cutoff at its measured value of 90 costs the calls on three Chirohepevirus genomes that scored 62.6, 77.2 and 84.9 — a price paid deliberately to remove the module's only duplicate call.

Paslahepevirus ORF4 is declared nowhere. It is a real protein with a real paper behind it (the HEV-1 ER-stress factor, PMID 27035822), but BV-BRC holds two unique sequences, and two sequences cannot train a profile at any threshold. It is retired to NOT_MODELLED.tsv rather than shipped as a feature that would never fire.

There are no derived features in this module, and that is a finding rather than an omission. The step 6b collinearity test was run on one reference genome per genus: every ORF1, ORF2 and ORF3 returns exactly one tblastn HSP in a single reading frame. No ribosomal frameshift, no readthrough stop, no cleaved products, no SignalP. Contrast Closteroviridae in the same class, where the identical test returns two HSPs in frames 2 and 3 — a +1 frameshift that will need a transcript_edit reference set.

01What the module covers

One genus — canine, feline and porcine parvovirus, minute virus of mice and their relatives — with seven features and two of them spliced.

Protoparvovirus is the only genus in the module. Seven features are declared: NS1, NS2, NS3, NP1, SAT, and the capsid pair VP1 and VP2.

NS2 and NS3 are declared special: splice and are called from curated nucleotide references by get_splice_variant_features.pl rather than by a profile. A spliced feature is never treated as essential.

The literature settles NS2. The splice junctions of minute virus of mice were mapped in 1986 (PMID 2942705); NS2 shares its first exon with NS1 and then reads in a different frame. The transcription and translation map of the NADL-2 strain of porcine parvovirus (PMID 8212598) predicted a third product, NS3, from a 2.9 kb transcript, and that is the source this module cites for it — a prediction, not a confirmed protein.

SAT is the other oddity: a small alternatively translated protein read from a second frame inside the capsid gene, which ends up in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane. It is not spliced and is called from a profile.

A hundred and seventeen distinct annotation strings collapse onto the seven feature names, over 6,366 source sequences. NS1 draws on thirty-two of them, VP1 on twenty-four, NS2 on twenty-four, VP2 on twenty-three.

Nine strings did not collapse, twelve sequences in all. One of them names a real thing the module does not model: nonstructural protein NS2P. NS2 exists as two isoforms, NS2P and NS2Y, differing at the C-terminus by a small second intron — and the splice caller models one intron, so the module calls one NS2 and does not distinguish them.

NS2's 187 and NS3's 8 nucleotide references replace what used to be their profiles. Each is a genomic slice with its splice donor and acceptor marked.

The PSSM floors are VP1 37, NP1 84, VP2 88, SAT 117 and NS1 148 bits. NS2 and NS3 carry no bit cutoff, no coverage cutoff and no extension flags, because none of those describes how a splice feature is called.

The splice references were not guessed, and getting them was the hard part of this build. Protoparvovirus NS2 splices at a GC–AG donor, not the canonical GT–AG, at an intron of 1,472 nucleotides. A brute-force search over every donor and acceptor pair in the 24 genomes that have an NS2 deposit reproduced the deposited protein byte-for-byte in 22 of them, all at that junction. NS3's 8 references come from its own deposits, at an intron of 1,839 nucleotides, every one reproducing its genome's deposit exactly.

Self-recall against each feature's own in-range collection: 2106 of 2161 (97.5%) — NS1 776/802, NP1 24/24, SAT 17/17, VP 759/760, VP2 530/558. This is a floor, not a validation; the genome-level figures are in §05 and §06.

07What it cannot do

Four limits, each measured rather than supposed.

NS2P and NS2Y are not distinguished. The two isoforms differ by a small second intron near the C-terminus, and get_splice_variant_features.pl models one intron. The module calls an NS2; which isoform it is, it does not say.

NS3 is a prediction. Its only literature source reports a 2.9 kb transcript that would encode a 12.4 kDa protein, and notes that no such protein had been described. Twenty-six deposits exist, from three taxa. The module calls it where the junction can be placed and is silent elsewhere; neither a call nor its absence should be read as evidence about the protein's existence.

VP1's bit floor is 37. That is the lowest in the family and it is what the collection supports, but it means VP1 will fire on weak similarity. Check a VP1 call's length against the 420–1,093 window before trusting it.

NP1 is declared on 26 deposits under three strings, one of which is nonstructural protein 2. NP1 and NS2 are slots in a genome layout, not names of proteins. The binning is by sequence, but the evidence base is thin and the feature is named uncharacterized NP1-like protein for that reason.

Use this module for protoparvovirus genomes; NS1, VP1, VP2 and NS2 are reliable. Treat NS3 as a prediction the module will confirm or stay silent about, and do not read an NS2 call as identifying NS2P or NS2Y.

08What the run found

What the build turned up that was not expected.

NS3 was being called at 669 residues against a declared maximum of 113, on exactly NS1's coordinates, in 210 of 299 genomes. The collection was clean — NS3 runs 79 to 107 residues across 26 deposits — so this was not the capsid mislabelling that affected other modules in this family. NS2 and NS3 share their first exon with NS1, so a short profile necessarily matches inside the NS1 open reading frame, and the start and stop extension then walked the call out to NS1's own boundaries.

Finding the real junction took three attempts and the first two looked healthy. The default nudge window of 12 nucleotides gave 1 reference; widening it to 24 gave 162, with a product 10 residues short of the deposit; widening to 48 gave 177, one residue short. All of them had a canonical GT–AG junction, a clean translation and the right length class. None of them was right. Only comparing the spliced product to that genome's own deposited protein separated the GC–AG donor from the GT that sits beside it.

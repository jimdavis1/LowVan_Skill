The mislabel

BV-BRC calls the allexivirus 40K protein "TGB3" in 127 of 141 sequences.

They are different proteins and differ four-fold in length.

A rule mapping TGB3 → TGB3 on the wording would have put 127 sequences of a 364-residue protein into a feature whose real members are 98 residues. The length distribution is what separates them, and it separates them cleanly — there is no overlap at all between 93–98 and 356–380.

This also explains an absence that was initially read as evidence the gene does not exist. The dump contains only 5 genuine Allexivirus TGB3 proteins against 179 TGB1 and 125 TGB2, because most of what carries the TGB3 label in this genus is the 40K protein, and the real TGB3 has no AUG for an ORF caller to find.

The 40K protein gets the uncharacterized string rather than a functional claim. It is allexivirus-specific, variously called a 40 kDa protein, a serine-rich protein and P42 in the source, and no citation a curator has read establishes what it does. The community name lives in the gene symbol, where it belongs, and the annotation makes no claim the evidence does not support.

Annotation strings are drawn from the existing controlled vocabulary; this module adds none. Symbols follow the rhabdovirus precedent already in the repository — replicase is L, coat protein is N.

01What the module covers

One genus, and the first module in this family in which two features are called by splicing rather than by a profile.

Amdoparvovirus is the only genus in the module; Aleutian mink disease virus is its type species. Five features are declared: NS1, NS2, NS3, and the capsid pair VP1 and VP2.

NS1 and VP1 carry copy_num. NS2 and NS3 do not, and cannot — they are declared special: splice and called from curated nucleotide references by get_splice_variant_features.pl, and a spliced feature is never treated as essential.

The literature is settled on this. AMDV transcribes from a single promoter and makes its small nonstructural proteins by alternative processing of the pre-mRNA (PMID 16378968); the abundant R2 message is tricistronic, carrying NS2 alongside VP1 and VP2 (PMID 17428872); and both NS2 and NS3 were confirmed to be expressed during infection, with NS3 at much lower levels (PMID 24606679).

Forty-four distinct annotation strings collapse onto the five feature names, over 841 source sequences. VP2 draws on eleven of them, NS2 on ten, NS1 and NS3 on eight each.

NS2's ten are the warning sign that started this build's hardest thread. They include nonstructural protein 3, NS3 and non-structural protein 3 alongside the NS2 spellings. The same protein is deposited under both names by different submitters, and the reverse is also true in the NS3 collection. That is not cosmetic: 28 deposits of AMDV's NS3 are labelled NS2, which put them in the wrong collection and is the root of the NS3 defect described in §07. They are binned as NS3 now.

The three PSSM features — NS1, VP1 and VP2 — keep nine profiles between them. NS2 and NS3 have none: they are called from 227 and 60 nucleotide references, each a genomic slice with its splice donor and acceptor marked.

The PSSM floors are NS1 709, VP1 1,028 and VP2 1,056 bits. NS2 and NS3 carry no bit cutoff, no coverage cutoff and no extension flags, because none of those describes how a splice feature is called — a bit cutoff on a feature with no profile reads as a threshold someone chose and invites the next curator to tune it.

The splice references were not guessed. NS2's 227 references come from a two-exon build whose spliced product is 114 residues in 209 of them, and 47 reproduce their own genome's deposited NS2 exactly. NS3's 60 were rebuilt in November against the deposits themselves: each one's junction is placed so that the spliced translation is that genome's own deposited protein, so all 60 reproduce their deposit by construction. The intron is 1,352 nucleotides in 57 of them.

Self-recall against each feature's own in-range collection: 427 of 427 (100.0%) — NS1 167/167, VP 115/115, VP2 145/145. This is a floor, not a validation; the genome-level figures are in §05 and §06.

VP1 and NS1 clear the 0.85 bar and carry copy_num; VP2 clears it on the raw rate but never stands alone — every VP2 call sits inside a VP1 call, which is what a nested alternative start looks like — so it is blocked from being marked essential.

NS3 was 15.9% in the first build, and that figure was an artefact, not biology. The page then explained it as low expression and sparse deposition. The real cause was that AMDV's NS3 is 87 residues — 60 shared with NS1 and NS2, 27 unique (PMID 24606679) — while the feature declared max_len 80, and every AMDV reference encoded a truncated splice acceptor giving 66 to 68 residues. The protein was excluded by six residues, in the genus's own type species. Re-binning the deposits, widening the window to 59–95 and rebuilding the references moved NS3 from 46 of 300 genomes to 217, and in Aleutian mink disease virus from 10 of 242 to 171. The called protein is now 92 residues in 145 genomes and 87 in 24; before the rebuild the longest NS3 called anywhere in the module was 72.

07What it cannot do

Three limits, each measured rather than supposed.

The third spliced product is identified, and it was NS3. The genus uses three acceptors off one donor. The first build read them as NS2 at 114 residues, NS3 at 66 to 71, and an undeclared product of 87 or 92 residues deposited 28 times as NS2 and 4 times as NS3. The third was not a third protein. Huang et al. give AMDV NS3 as 87 amino acids, 60 shared with NS1 and NS2 and 27 unique (PMID 24606679), and name no fourth small nonstructural protein. The 87/92 residue product is AMDV's own NS3 at the published length; the 66 to 71 residue form is a truncated annotation of the same reading frame. No AMDV genome deposits both. Those deposits are now binned as NS3 and the references rebuilt from them.

NS3 is called in three genomes in four, and the gap is reference coverage, not biology. Its 60 references are what could be placed against sequence. Three small taxa get no NS3 at all — 1908805, 2734306 and 1093101, three genomes or fewer each — and two 1513315 genomes that were called before are not called now, because that taxon's references fell from 11 to 5 when the rebuild dropped every deposit whose genome has no contig file. A genome whose NS3 junction differs from all 60 gets no call and no warning. Do not read an absent NS3 as evidence that the genome lacks the gene.

One reference contig. Routing has no redundancy here at all.

Use this module for amdoparvovirus genomes. NS1, VP1 and VP2 are reliable, and NS3 now calls the protein the literature describes rather than a truncated form of it. Treat an NS2 or NS3 call as a positive finding and their absence as no finding: both are placed from nucleotide references, so a genome without a close enough reference is passed over silently.

08What the run found

What the build turned up that was not expected.

This module is the reason the family's NS features were re-examined at all. Applying copy_num dropped it to 7 of 300 genomes clean: NS2 was flagged "too many HSPs" in 288, and NS1, NS2 and NS3 sat on identical coordinates in 274. The call rate and the self-recall were perfect throughout, so nothing before that step looked wrong.

The cause was not the collections, which are clean — NS2 runs 80 to 116 residues across 145 deposits. It was that NS2 and NS3 share their first exon with NS1, so a short profile necessarily matches inside the NS1 open reading frame, and the start and stop extension then walked the call out to NS1's own boundaries. The printed NS2 translation was byte-for-byte NS1's: 600 residues against a declared maximum of 122.

Two things had to be true before that was visible. A feature's declared length window is never checked against a PSSM-called feature, and the test repository was running a caller eight weeks older than the kit's, in which the coverage cutoff was measured against the matched span rather than the profile — so it was always about 1.0 and never fired. Both are now recorded; the fix that shipped is the splice declaration, not a change to either.

The rebuild that followed is the better lesson. NS3 was called in 46 of 300 genomes and the audit explained that as biology — lower expression, fewer submitters. It was an artefact: 28 of the 32 correctly sized deposits are labelled NS2 by their submitters, so they binned into NS2 and were never available to build NS3, which was then built from two truncated annotations and given a max_len of 80. Unlike a PSSM call, a splice feature does enforce its length window, so AMDV's 87-residue NS3 was excluded by six residues. The measurement had been reported honestly and explained wrongly, which is the harder failure to catch.

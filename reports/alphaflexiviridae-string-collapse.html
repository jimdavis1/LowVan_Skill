Where the 275 come from

Almost none of it is disagreement about biology. The three triple gene block proteins alone account for 145 of the 275 strings for three genes, and the variation is orthographic rather than semantic:

  • the TGBp1 / TGB1 / TGB1 protein / triple gene block protein 1 / triple gene block 1 family, in every combination of spacing, case and the trailing word "protein"
  • putative as a free-floating prefix, doubling many strings without changing the claim
  • kilodalton sizes embedded in names — 23kDa, 23 kDa, 23KDa — which are three spellings of one number and pin the string to one virus's protein size
  • plain typographic noise

A consumer matching on strings sees 275 proteins where there are six. That is the cost LowVan removes.

NABP is the feature to read sceptically. Only 118 features carry it in this module, and four of its twelve strings embed "23 kDa", which is a Potexvirus-specific size. Its profiles are also still trained 338:54 on Allexivirus sequences inherited from before the split, for genomes that now route elsewhere. The feature is genuine but thin, and it is called on 5 of 170 genomes.

The annotation records what the protein does; the short community name lives in gene_symbol. So the replicase is "RNA-dependent RNA polymerase" with symbol L, never "L protein". No taxon prefix appears in any string — that convention was removed from the vocabulary in the current revision.

This module adds nothing new to the controlled vocabulary: all six strings already existed in it before this build.

A note on what is not here

ALLEXI_40K was declared by this module until Allexivirus was split out. It is 100% Allexivirus and its genomes now route elsewhere, so the feature was removed rather than left declared and uncallable. Its source strings — and the discovery that BV-BRC labels that protein "TGB3" in 127 sequences of median 364 aa — are documented in the Allexivirus collapse instead.

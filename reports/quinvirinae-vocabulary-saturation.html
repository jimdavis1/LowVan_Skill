Quinvirinae

A controlled vocabulary stops growing. Free text does not.

A Quinvirinae genome encodes the same 8 modelled proteins as any other, so a curated vocabulary needs exactly 8 names no matter how many genomes you read. Free-text product strings have no such ceiling: each new submitter spells the same proteins a new way. Sampling 145 annotated genomes 100 times over shows the two curves doing exactly that — the module is saturated after 11 genomes, while the source vocabulary is still climbing at 145.

The ratio is the number worth quoting: at the end of each curve, free text costs 33.8 strings per 100 genomes and the controlled vocabulary 5.5 — a 6.1-fold collapse. Reaching 95% of its final size takes the source vocabulary 124 genomes (86% of the set); the module gets there on genome 11.

Does the Togaviridae module work?

Thirteen features — two alphavirus polyproteins, the nine mature peptides that tile them, and two multi-product intermediates — measured against every Togaviridae genome BV-BRC holds between 1 and 50 kb. 13,032 records collapse to 679 clusters at 95% nucleotide identity, 387 of which have a near-complete member. 01What the module covers

Togaviridae is monogeneric — ICTV moved Rubivirus to Matonaviridae — and every alphavirus has one genome organisation:

5'-[ nsP1 - nsP2 - nsP3 - nsP4 ]---(junction)---[ C - E3 - E2 - 6K - E1 ]-3'
     nonstructural polyprotein                    structural polyprotein

A module is a group of taxa that share a gene layout, so Togaviridae is exactly one module. Salmonid alphavirus is the divergent end of the genus and was checked rather than assumed — same nine proteins, same order — so it shares this module and gets its own reference contig.

227 genomes that BV-BRC files under Togaviridae are not togaviruses, and they are excluded by species with the evidence recorded: eighteen 10-segment orbiviruses annotated VP1–VP7, eight 3-segment bunyaviruses annotated N/M/L, and 29 fungal and invertebrate viruses carrying only an alphavirus-like superfamily replicase and none of the structural cassette.

The exclusion holds where it matters

No reference contig routes any of the 227. They are rejected before annotation rather than captured and dead-ended — which is the failure mode a reference contig for an uncovered taxon creates, and the reason one was not added.

02How it was built

Binned by annotation string and checked by length, homology and subject coverage. Three of the thirteen — full-length nsP3, pE2 and P123 — are not binned from the export at all: they are recovered from the genome interval between their flanking features, because the source holds almost nothing for them (8 pE2 sequences, 2 P123) and, for nsP3, because the profiles could not reach past the opal.

Declared partials never train. BV-BRC marks incomplete products explicitly (…, fragment, …, C-terminal, truncated …), which is better evidence than any length window and catches what a window cannot: 89 Ross River records annotated “putative non-structural polyprotein precursor P1234, C-terminal” are 402–450 aa, comfortably inside any nsP3 range wide enough to hold Wenling fish alphavirus nsP3 at 421 aa. They were being re-homed into the nsP3 collection at 98% identity, where they would have taught it two wrong termini.

Self-recall is a floor, not a validation. A profile that cannot recover the sequences it was built from will certainly recover nothing new; recovering them proves very little. It is in the table because a number below 99% would be a finding.

The binning is corroborated by something no rule looked at

The modal first residue of each collection:

C     M 96%     E3    S 79%             E2    S 86%     6K  A/E/D     E1  Y 91%

nsP4's invariant N-terminal tyrosine and nsP3's invariant alanine are the documented nsP2-protease sites; E2's serine is the furin site in PE2; E1's tyrosine and 6K's small residue are signal-peptidase sites; nsP1 and C start at their precursor's own initiating Met. A mis-binned collection does not produce that.

03Where the cutoffs sit

A bit_cutoff has to sit above the best score a profile reaches on sequence that is not the feature, and below the weakest score it reaches on sequence that is. In a polyprotein taxon the first of those needs care: every mature peptide is a substring of its precursor, so a hit to the precursor's coordinates is the feature and must not count as noise.

E3 (64 aa) and 6K (60 aa) are short enough that the window is narrow: 26–63 and 25–48 bits. The direct test of whether cutoffs of 45 and 38 produce false positives is how often a feature declared copy_num: 1 gets called twice, and across 383 annotated genomes E3, 6K, C and nsP1 are the four cleanest features in the module at 0%. The multi-copy tail sits on the long features — nsP4 17%, SP 9% — where it is one gene fragmented in a broken assembly, not a spurious second call.

Measured over all 13,032 downloaded genomes at the annotator's own -min_contig_bit 150, with the annotator's own BLASTn parameters: 12,796 of 12,805 modelled genomes route, the nine misses all 1,005–3,883 bp partials. Over the 387 near-complete exemplars, 385 route and the two that do not are the two excluded species. The routing checker disagreed, and it was wrong

check_rep_contigs.py invokes blastn with no parameters, so it runs megablast (reward 1, penalty −2, word_size 28); the annotator routes with -reward 2 -penalty -3 -word_size 11 -soft_masking false, which is far more sensitive across a divergent genus. It reported four RefSeq alphaviruses as unroutable — Middelburg and Southern elephant seal virus with no hit at all — and advised adding a reference contig for each.

Running the annotator on those four routes them at 654–1697 bits and calls all eleven features in every one. Four references would have been added to fix nothing. Measure with the search you will actually use.

05Does it call the proteins?

In complete genomes the core proteins should appear in near-equal numbers, because every genome has one of each. A protein lagging its neighbours is either absent or invisible to the profiles, and those demand opposite responses.

Nine features sit within five points of each other at 327–370%. Two trail: 6K by 12 points and the nonstructural polyprotein by 18. Neither reaches the 20-point threshold that mandates an investigation, and both were investigated anyway.

Both gaps are genome quality, and that is measured, not assumed

genomes WITH 6K                    7.4%              69%

A 6K is 183 nucleotides; an N run over it removes the protein. A polyprotein needs 65% coverage of a 2,474-column profile, which a genome that is 26% ambiguous cannot supply. Of the 42 genomes that called both of 6K's neighbours but not 6K, 42 of 42 have ambiguous bases in the interval, and the twelve that yield an ORF yield one of 132–138 aa against a 6K of 55–68. Those are not 6K, and they were deliberately not merged into the collection.

06Are the calls right?

Coverage says a protein was called. Whether it was called correctly is a different question, and three checks answer it.

Held-out reference panel

39 genomes | reference CDS 107 | called 117 | reference CDS MISSED 0 | duplicates 1

Not one of the 107 reference CDS is missed. That is the coverage statement, and it is the one this panel exists to make — the tool's own docstring says it is there to find "which genes the module misses and where it doubles up, not to match GenBank."

An earlier version of this page quoted 73% from the same evaluation. That figure is not a coverage rate and should not be read as one. evaluate_module.py accumulates matches into a set of call indices and stops at the first call overlapping each reference CDS, so when several reference CDS overlap one call they collapse to a single entry. 78 is therefore the number of distinct calls claimed by some reference, not the number of reference CDS matched — and the number missed, which is the quantity 73% was being read as the complement of, is zero.

The 39 unclaimed calls resolve by arithmetic rather than investigation: 117 = 39 × 3 (nsP1234, nsP123, STP) and 78 = 39 × 2, so exactly one call per genome goes unclaimed. It is the second member of the overlapping nsP1234/nsP123 pair: the reference annotates one nonstructural CDS, this module emits two features across it, and a one-to-one match cannot claim the same reference twice. Nothing is wrong with either call.

The panel compares CDS features only, so of the fourteen features declared it sees three. TF is a mat_peptide and does not enter this evaluation at all, nor do the nine mature products — so these numbers are silent about TF in either direction. The reference's 18 join(...) CDS for the frameshift product are covered by the TF call on the same interval, but as a mat_peptide against a CDS they could not register here whatever the module did.

The one duplicate is recorded, not smoothed over. NC_043402 carries two in-frame stops in its nonstructural ORF — the opal at codon 1916 and the genuine terminator at 2530 — with the nsP1234 match overshooting the terminator so both fall inside it. Readthrough is then refused and the call cropped back to the opal, landing on P123's coordinates. Raising the internal-stop cap does not fix it: it reads through the real terminator instead, to 7650 where the protein ends at 7644. One genome in 39, treated as an outlier.

This settles the one place where the schema's rule and a later measurement disagree. nsP4 and E1 are the last product of their precursor, and their C-termini are not protease sites — nothing marks them but the stop codon. Given downstream_ext: 1, nsP4's end equals the precursor's end in 100% of opal-free genomes and E1's equals the structural polyprotein's in 92%.

The residual is the same population as every other shortfall here. Splitting the 263 clean genomes at each boundary into crisp (within ±3 nt) and creeping, the creeping ones carry two to two and a half times the ambiguous-base content at every one of the five boundaries — 10.6–14.9% against 5.1–6.1%. The offsets are hundreds to thousands of nucleotides, a feature placed elsewhere in a broken assembly, not a cleavage site mis-declared.

07What it cannot do

Most alphaviruses carry an in-frame opal (UGA) codon six codons before the nsP3/nsP4 cleavage site — inside nsP3, not between the two proteins.

It is a genuine stop, not a codon the ribosome ignores. Termination there is the normal outcome and yields P123; readthrough occurs about 10% of the time, inserting an amino acid at the opal and continuing in the same frame to yield P1234 (Li, Sun, Tuplin & Harris 2023, the one human-read DLIT in this module). Because the opal sits inside nsP3, that readthrough is required for full-length nsP3 as well as for nsP4 — which is what makes this a two-protein problem rather than a polymerase one. Neither that paper nor GenBank commits to which residue is inserted; GenBank writes /transl_except=(pos:…,aa:OTHER) and renders it X.

Who has it was measured rather than assumed. The annotator crops any match at an internal stop, so on an opal genome the nsP3 call ends on the opal — the three bases ending at nsP3's called end are TGA — and there is then a gap of exactly 18 nt before nsP4 begins. On an opal-free genome that codon is nsP3's real last codon and nsP4 starts immediately. Requiring both signals to agree is what makes the test safe; the codon alone gives false positives in broken assemblies, where the crop happens at a sequencing error instead. The same locus in two Chikungunya strains:

opal       ...gagttatga | CTAGACAGGGCAGGTGGG | tatata...   nsP3 ends on TGA, gap 18
no opal    ...gcaggtggg | TATATATTCTCGTCGGAC | accggt...   nsP3 runs through, gap 0

Identical six codons, identical nsP4 start (TAT ATA, the invariant Tyr-Ile). The only difference is the codon before them, and in the opal-free genomes it across the cleanly-callable opal-free genomes is CGA ×5, AGA ×2, CAA ×5, TGT ×1 — arginine in 7 of 13, by AGA or CGA, exactly the two codons Li et al. report for opal-free isolates, both one substitution from UGA. The five CAA are every salmonid alphavirus cluster: a glutamine, and a lineage-wide state rather than a passage artefact.

Five species are polymorphic for it, which is the expected pattern: the opal is lost on laboratory passage, so the prototype strains in RefSeq under-represent it relative to field isolates. 85 of 275 exemplars could not be read at all (median 15.7% ambiguous bases) and are excluded from both figures rather than guessed at.

The annotator cannot emit the readthrough form, and that was checked in its source

Two independent mechanisms prevent it. The PSSM path crops a hit at an internal stop; the coordinate-anchored path refuses any derived protein containing one at all. So declaring P1234 as a feature anchored from nsP1's start to nsP4's stop — the obvious fix, and the mechanism Arenaviridae GP1 uses — produces nothing on exactly the genomes that need it. -keep_stop is a global flag, not a per-feature one.

So the module declares one feature for the nonstructural ORF, bounded to span both products and annotated Nonstructural polyprotein, which claims neither. Naming it … P1234 would be wrong for 86% of the genus.

One cost is recorded rather than hidden: on opal genomes the ORF is emitted as P123. nsP3 is no longer short. It was, by seven residues — the profiles could not read past the opal — and the fix was to rebuild every nsP3 profile from the end of nsP2 to the start of nsP4 so the readthrough is inside the training data. GenBank's own readthrough translation of Sindbis reads …SRRTEY [X] LTGVGG YIFSTD…, giving a mature readthrough nsP3 of 549 + 1 + 6 = 556 aa. This module now emits 556, ending …SRRTEY*LTGVGG with nsP4 starting YIFSTDTG… — the opal at residue 550 and six residues after it, on every opal genome checked. The opal-free salmonid alphaviruses carry no internal stop at all and are unaffected, as is nsP4, called from its own profiles at the invariant Tyr in both genome types.

The 18 nt by which P123 falls short of nsP3's end is not a truncation: P123 is the opal-terminated product, so it ends at the opal, while nsP3 is the readthrough mature peptide and runs six codons further. Two different proteins, two different C-termini.

Separating P123 from P1234 needs a readthrough-aware caller, which is an annotator change, not a module change.

Two products the export could not supply

Both are now built, neither from the source annotation, because the source does not hold enough of either to train on:

  • p62/PE2, the E3+E2 precursor — exactly one full-length sequence in the whole export; the seven records annotated “E3-E2 envelope protein” are 110–247 aa partials. Recovered instead from the genome interval between the E3 and E2 calls, and profiled from that.
  • TF, the transframe protein — four sequences of 49–81 aa in the export, of which two are demonstrably 6K read through into E1 in frame 0, so the source could not even check the work. No profile can call it in any case: a −1 frameshift product is not collinear with the genome, and tblastn splits a TF query into two HSPs in different frames. It is declared special: transcript_edit with no PSSM and reconstructed from 234 curated nucleotide references that each carry one extra templated base. Called on 293 of 381 genomes (77%) by the real pipeline, with 9 more falling to the Uncorrected transFrame protein encoding region partial — the graceful-degradation path for a genome 80–95% identical to its nearest reference, close enough to locate the site but not to carry the correction.

The rule that kept both unbuilt for as long as it did still holds: declaring a feature with nothing behind it is worse than not declaring it, because the annotator reports a declared-but-unbuilt feature as missing on every genome and never says why. The fix is to build it, not to declare it hopefully.

TF calls that should not have been made

Of the 293 TF calls, 263 are clean; 31 carry an X and one carries an internal stop. That is not the reference set's doing — every one of the 234 references was screened, and 42 candidate genomes were dropped at build time precisely because their slip site was N-masked. It is the calling side. get_transcript_edited_features.pl applies four gates — identity, coverage, gap count, gap runs — then translates whatever the gap-fill produces and stores it without ever inspecting the protein. The filled sequence takes its bases from the subject genome, so an N-masked target yields an X-bearing protein and the annotator emits it anyway.

So two honest figures exist and this page gives both rather than picking one: 293 (77%) called, 263 (69%) called cleanly. The second is the one to quote where sequence quality matters. Screening the translation for X and internal stops belongs in the annotator, where it would apply to every transcript-edited feature in every module, not in this module alone.

Two genomes carry two TF calls apiece — 37124.9707 at 9735 and 13984, 59301.286 at 9648 and 21083, each pair identical in length. Both are assembly artefacts: the contig holds the genome twice over, so the locus is genuinely present twice. Not a module defect.

23.5% is the wrong number to quote, in both directions. The separation on ambiguity is total: every one of the 90 unflagged genomes has a median N content of 0.00%, and 273 of the 293 flagged ones carry more than 1% ambiguous bases. A genome that is 10–20% N cannot present a full protein set; flagging it is the quality tool working, not the module failing. The flag itself under-reports this, being a contig-level test — of the 94 poor genomes that never receive it, 74 still exceed 1% N genome-wide.

08What the run found

A whole-taxon run is worth doing for what it turns up as much as for what it scores. None of the following was visible from the reference panel.

Four per virus, each containing not one cytosine. The structural ORF of 11020.84 translates to 57 stop codons where the RefSeq copy of the same virus at the same length translates to one. The annotator rejects them correctly and records the routing decision so the taxon is not lost.

One matters beyond the count. 11039.148 was the third pick in a greedy reference-contig set cover run over all near-complete exemplars — selecting references by coverage alone would have installed a genome with no cytosine as a routing reference. The nine that shipped are RefSeq genomes chosen for provenance, which is why that did not happen.

Four fabricated citations

An early pass put five plausible-looking PMIDs into the module JSON. They resolved to a bacterial cytoskeleton paper, an octopus haemocyanin cDNA and a peptide synthetase; check_dlits.py caught all of them. Every id now present was checked to resolve to the paper it is named for — and all twelve stay flagged PMID_claude_generated, because resolving an id is not reading the paper. That flag is why no annotation string in this module makes a functional claim the source data does not already make: nsP2 is “Protease nsP2 protein” and not “Protease and RNA helicase”, and nsP3 makes no claim at all.

Three defects in the build kit

  • build_leftover_pssms.py aligns its sequences to build the profile but writes the unaligned ones to the file recorded beside it as its alignment. 22 of 66 leftover files were affected; those profiles could never have been rebuilt, and the files would have shipped as provenance.
  • rebuild_pssms.py reports every pipeline-built profile as stale — 130 of 174, none of them edited — because its rebuild omits a metadata block the pipeline's own builder adds after psiblast.
  • check_rep_contigs.py routes with megablast defaults rather than the annotator's parameters, as described in section 04.

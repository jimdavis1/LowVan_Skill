Does the cardiovirus module work?

Cardiovirus holds encephalomyocarditis virus (Cardiovirus A, C. rueckerti), Theiler's murine encephalomyelitis virus (Cardiovirus B, C. theileri) and Saffold virus (Cardiovirus D, C. saffoldi), the one of the three isolated from humans. Theiler's virus is also the standard animal model of multiple sclerosis. This audit covers the module built for the genus. It was extended on 5 October with the two proteins cardioviruses make out of frame with their own polyprotein, L* and 2B*, which the first version did not call.

01What the module covers

One genus, one layout, and fifteen features — two of them out of frame with the polyprotein.

The cardiovirus polyprotein is leader, VP4, VP2, VP3, VP1, 2A, 2B, 2C, 3A, VPg, 3C, 3D. The leader is a 67–76 residue zinc-finger protein, median 71, that antagonises host nucleocytoplasmic trafficking, not a protease: it is a different protein from the 201-residue aphthovirus leader protease that occupies the same slot, which is why the two genera are separate modules.

Two further proteins are made from the same RNA and are not part of the polyprotein's reading frame. L* is translated from an alternative AUG in the leader region, out of frame. 2B* is a transframe fusion made by −1 ribosomal frameshifting inside 2B. The module's first version declared neither; both are now built, and sections 02 and 05 report how they were made and how often they are called.

Every collection was large enough to build at -m 5 -mi 0.8. The thinnest are VPg at 22 sequences and 3A at 36, which still cleared the floor, so no feature needed a departure and no cut site had to be projected from polyproteins.

L* is an ordinary PSSM feature. An overlapping ORF in a different reading frame is still found by tblastn, which translates the genome in all six frames, so nothing special was required — only a collection. Searching 209 annotated genomes for an out-of-frame ORF of at least 100 codons starting at an AUG within 150 nucleotides of the polyprotein start gives 47 sequences, median 156 residues, which build into 7 profiles. It is declared a CDS with kmers: 0, because the region it occupies is not clean — the polyprotein runs through it in another frame.

2B* cannot be a PSSM at all, because it is not collinear with the genome. It is declared special: transcript_edit with a set of per-genome nucleotide references, the same mechanism the alphavirus TransFrame protein uses. Each reference is built by reading the genome to the slippery heptamer, duplicating the slipped base and continuing in the shifted frame to the stop. The heptamer is in phase and intact in 80 of 209 genomes, every one of them C. rueckerti (EMCV), with the slip at codon 8 in 77 of them.

The resulting reference set checks out on every count the method requires: all 80 translations end in a stop, none has an internal stop, none contains an ambiguous base, and the lengths are 129 residues in 76 cases, 130 in three and 131 in one — against a published length of 128–129. The N-terminus reads PFMAKPKKQVF…, which is 2B's own start: the eleven to twelve shared residues the frameshift papers describe, reproduced without being imposed. A self round-trip through the full annotator chain recovers the protein byte-identically on 12 of 12 donor genomes.

0 of 106 routed exemplars get every one of the 13 core features. No core feature trails the best-called one by 20 points or more. Excluded from that count: 2BSTAR, LSTAR — called by an external program and absent from this table, or a protein part of the genus does not encode.

The lowest are VPg at 97, and 2A and 3A at 98. VPg is 22 residues, so it sits on the 20-bit floor where the length window does most of the discrimination; the nine genomes that miss it are not concentrated in any one species. The leader is called on 101, which for a protein this short and this variable across the genus is the expected shape rather than a coverage hole.

L* is called on 43 of 106 exemplars, and the distribution is the test. It is called on 23 of 53 C. theileri, all 8 C. ranori, all 4 rodent cardioviruses and all 2 C. dhusarah — and on 1 of 26 C. rueckerti. That is the published picture: L* is a Theilovirus protein carried by the TO subgroup and absent from the GDVII strains, and encephalomyocarditis virus does not make one. A feature that fired on every genome would have been evidence of a bad profile, not of good coverage.

2B* does not appear in this section, and that is a property of the measurement rather than the module. evaluate_coverage.py reads the annotator's flat feature table, which has no representation for a special feature. 2B* is written to the GTO instead, so any per-feature rate derived from the table would report it as absent. It was verified separately by the round-trip in section 02.

07What it cannot do

What remains after the two out-of-frame proteins were built.

2B* is covered only for encephalomyocarditis virus. The 80 references are all C. rueckerti, because that is where the long +2 ORF exists. Theiloviruses carry the same conserved heptamer sixteen nucleotides before the called 2B start and frameshift at it with very high efficiency, but into a much shorter ORF. Whether that short product should be declared as a feature is a judgement about what counts as a protein worth calling, and it has not been made here.

The 2B* reference set is dense but narrow. The correction gate is 95% nucleotide identity, so a reference only serves close relatives. 80 references drawn from one species will not reach a divergent EMCV, and the annotator degrades gracefully rather than failing — it emits an uncorrected coding region with no translation. That is the right behaviour and it is still an uncalled protein.

L* is called on fewer than half the exemplars, and that is expected rather than a gap. The ones without it are overwhelmingly EMCV and the GDVII-type theiloviruses, which do not encode it. Distinguishing "absent because the virus does not have it" from "absent because the profile missed it" would need the ORF search re-run on the uncalled genomes; the extraction already reports no qualifying ORF in those, which is consistent but is not an independent check.

Nineteen source strings bind to no feature, and after this build five of them are no longer a gap: the strings naming L* and 2B* now have features behind them. The rest are ordinary names whose sequences were dropped by the length and ambiguity filters, which collect_synmap.py cannot distinguish from strings that matched no rule.

Mupivirus routes here on 8 of 8 exemplars and has no module of its own. It receives 7.0 features per genome. Measured 6 October across all 404 genomes ≥6 kb of the 36 Picornaviridae genera that no module claims; of those 36, eighteen route into an existing module and collect between 3 and 14 features with no further work.

Both out-of-frame proteins are built and both are called where the biology says they should be. What is left is narrower: 2B* exists only for EMCV, its reference set reaches only close relatives, and the theilovirus short-ORF frameshift product is undeclared.

08What the run found

The run found two proteins that were never declared, and declaring them took two different mechanisms.

The first version of this module was correct about everything it declared and silent about two proteins it did not. No measurement of declared features can surface that: self-recall asks whether profiles find their own training data, the held-out panel scores whole genomes, and the cross-feature QC compares features that exist. A protein with no feature produces no wrong answer to flag — only a missing one that nothing is looking for.

What surfaced it was reading the genus literature and then going back to the sequence to check. Both proteins turned out to be measurable in data already on disk: the L* ORF by searching for an out-of-frame reading frame near the polyprotein start, and the 2B* frameshift by reading the slippery heptamer at its correct codon phase. The second is worth stating precisely, because phase is where this goes wrong: the heptamer is X_XXY_YYZ and its lone leading base is the third position of a codon, so the motif begins at phase 2 of the reading frame. Testing at phase 0 rejects every genome and looks like a finding.

Both were then validated against something not used to build them. L* reproduces its known species distribution, including near-absence from EMCV. 2B* reproduces its published length and its shared N-terminus with 2B, and round-trips byte-identically through the annotator on every donor genome tested.

Cardiovirus is better annotated than the duck or porcine genera and correspondingly more inconsistent: 97 distinct source strings collapse into 14 controlled ones. Drawn one protein at a time, the source vocabulary needs 711 of its 818 records to reach 95% of its final 70 strings and is still rising at the end; the module reaches 95% of its 13 after 38 proteins and adds nothing after that.

Nineteen source strings bind to no feature, and the two groups of them say different things. Most are ordinary names — polyprotein (36 records), protein 2A, capsid protein VP1 — that do match a rule but whose sequences were then dropped by the length and ambiguity filters, so they reach no collection. collect_synmap.py reads collection membership rather than rule matches, so a filtered record is indistinguishable here from an unmatched one; the count is a lower bound on what the vocabulary binds. The other group is not an artefact: five distinct strings name L* (L* protein, protein L*, L* peptide, L*) and one names protein 2B*. Those are no longer gaps: both features now exist, so the source data naming them has somewhere to bind. They remain listed as unbound here because this page's collapse figures were computed from the thirteen-feature collections, before L* and 2B* were added.

Does the Caenorhabditis nodavirus module actually work?

The strangest genomes in the family. These worm viruses encode neither B1 nor B2; their capsid begins at a codon that is not AUG; and a −1 ribosomal frameshift fuses that capsid to a second protein which also exists on its own, from its own start codon. Three overlapping products come out of one 2.3 kb stretch of RNA2.

01What the module covers

Four proteins, one of which has no profile at all.

Le Blanc nodavirus (49 genomes), Santeuil (44), Orsay (4) and Melnik (4). No ICTV genus covers them, so the module takes its most-sampled species' name. The margin is thin and worth stating: Orsay virus carries almost all of the literature below and has four genomes in BV-BRC.

RNA1 carries a single CDS. PMID 21283608 looked for a B2 and reported the absence directly: “All known nodavirus B2 proteins overlap with the C-terminus of the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase and are encoded in the +1 frame relative to the polymerase. No open reading frame with these properties was predicted in the 3′ end of the RNA1 segment.” PMID 23468484 then found no silencing-suppressor activity for either Orsay protein proposed as one.

02How it was built

98 training sequences, plus a nucleotide reference set for the protein that has no profile.

The capsid starts at a non-AUG codon. PMID 24503084 Edman-sequenced the mature Orsay capsid N-terminus as NKNNT and reports that it “corresponds to nucleotides 89–91, an AAC codon, which is not traditionally thought to be a suitable translation initiation site”, with “no AUG codon 5′ to” it in the segment. Checked directly on HM030971: nt 89–91 are AAC.

  verified on HM030971 (Orsay virus RNA2, 2574 nt)
     nt   89-  91   AAC      capsid start, non-AUG, Edman-confirmed
     nt 1255-1261   GTCAAAC  slippery heptamer
     nt 1262-1264   TAG      capsid stop, immediately 3' of the heptamer
     nt 1345-1347   ATG      free delta start

Consequences carried into the JSON rather than only into prose: upstream_ext is 0 on the capsid, because the annotator's scan_to_met_start would walk left looking for a start codon that does not exist; and check_met_start.py reports the feature in 100% of its members, which is the documented biology and not a defect. The initiation mechanism is explicitly unresolved in the literature and is not claimed here.

CP-delta is a −1 programmed ribosomal frameshift, so it has no PSSM. It is called from a curated nucleotide reference set in which one base is duplicated at the slip site. The transframe peptide was sequenced by MS/MS, and the protein incorporates into virions as a head fiber with a novel β-bracelet fold (PMIDs 24503084, 32817218, 28241071). RefSeq's join(89..1261,1261..2385) repeats nt 1261 because that is the slipped base — it is a frameshift, not a splice, and this module declares no spliced feature.

There are two slippery heptamers in this group, not one — and building on the published one alone covered half the module. With Orsay's G_UCA_AAC as the only constraint, 24 references were built from Le Blanc and Orsay, and 23 genomes reported no insertion sits in the slip site — every Santeuil and Melnik genome. Reading the 7 nucleotides immediately 5′ of the capsid stop in each genome separates them exactly by species: Orsay and Le Blanc carry GTCAAAC in 26 of 26; Santeuil and Melnik carry GGAAAAC in 24 of 24. PMID 24503084 notes only that Le Blanc shares Orsay's slippery sequence and Santeuil's differs, without giving Santeuil's.

Free delta is a separate initiation, and it is real. It has its own AUG at RNA2 nt 1345 and is 332–398 aa. PMID 29743360 settled it with an ATG→CTG mutant that still makes normal CP-delta but no free delta and has a non-lytic egress defect. It is absent from RefSeq NC_028098.1 and present only in HM030971.2, which is why only three deposits exist — the collection was expanded by extraction, and 46 of 46 extracted members are exact C-terminal substrings of the fusion from their own genome, beginning at fusion residue 419–434 (Orsay's published 420 among them).

CP-delta is called on every complete genome in the panel. That number cannot come from the flat feature table, which has no representation for a special feature at all — it comes from the full GTO pipeline, which runs the transcript-edit caller. A module with a frameshift product scored from the feature table would report that product as simply absent, with nothing to flag it.

Across all 65 genomes carrying any call, the replicase reaches 75% and the RNA2 products 77%, the shortfall being single-segment deposits that cannot carry the other segment's genes.

Every nesting reported is declared architecture: the capsid and free delta both sit inside the fusion, because the fusion is the capsid plus a frameshift plus delta. qc_cross_feature.py reports them as 100%-identical collisions, which is true by construction; audit_bins.py, which accepts a declaration, reads Unresolved: 0 with those pairs declared. The fusion itself has no profile, so there is no profile for the capsid to compete with.

07What it cannot do

Two real limits, one of them a direct consequence of how the deposits were made.

1. Santeuil and Melnik RNA2 deposits are 5′-truncated, and are excluded from the capsid collection. Their contigs begin inside the capsid gene, so the encoded proteins start at Lys rather than the Edman-verified Asn — 22 of 50 capsid deposits. Training a profile on them would place the capsid N-terminus 15 residues late in every genome it called. They are excluded rather than trimmed, which costs Santeuil representation in that one feature; its frameshift references are unaffected, because a reference only has to span the slip site.

2. The non-AUG start is annotated as a fact, not explained as a mechanism. The 2014 paper floats an upstream UUG at nt 56–58 followed by proteolysis, then argues against it — the UUG “lacks a strong initiation context” and Santeuil has an in-frame stop five codons upstream — and prefers initiation directly at the NKNNT site “via some unknown mechanism”. The module records the verified N-terminus and claims nothing about how the ribosome gets there.

3. Melnik nodavirus has no complete CDS and no RefSeq (MK774657–MK774660, two isolates, both ends open). It contributes a routing reference and two frameshift references, and it will not produce a complete annotation from the records that exist.

A trap defused rather than a limit. Santeuil's RefSeq NC_015070.1 labels the fusion protein delta and gives the capsid a /note="protein A". So one string covers both a 765 aa fusion and a ~346 aa free protein, and protein A appears on an RNA2 capsid. Length routing separates the first; protein A binds nothing in this module and is left to the homology rescue.

A, CP and DELTA carry copy_num. CP_DELTA deliberately does not, and never can: a special feature is emitted by a different program from the one that scores genome quality, so marking it essential would flag genomes on the basis of a measurement the quality step never makes.

One apparent miss was an artefact of merging. Santeuil 977913.15 appeared to lack the capsid, the fusion and delta — but it is MN131015 + MN131016, both 2,896 nt and both the polymerase, merged into a 5,792 nt “genome” with no capsid segment at all. It would have withheld copy_num from three features on the strength of an artefact. A length window cannot catch it, because this module's two segment windows are 2896–3628 and 2525–2937, so a pair of 2,896 nt polymerases satisfies both. Each contig is now assigned to a segment by homology to the module's own collections.

The collapse ratio is the lowest of the four modules, and for a good reason: this group was sequenced by a small number of laboratories over a short period, so its source vocabulary is already fairly disciplined. The fusion protein still arrives under two names — alpha-delta fusion protein and capsid-delta fusion protein — because submitters disagree about whether to call the capsid alpha.

Two strings are new to the LowVan vocabulary: Capsid-delta fusion protein and Nonlytic egress protein delta. The second names a function the literature establishes directly (PMID 29743360) rather than falling back on an uncharacterized form, and the first describes what the protein is rather than how it is made.

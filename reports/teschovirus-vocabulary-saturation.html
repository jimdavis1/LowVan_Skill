Teschovirus

A controlled vocabulary stops growing. Free text does not.

A Teschovirus genome encodes the same 13 modelled proteins as any other, so a curated vocabulary needs exactly 13 names no matter how many genomes you read. Free-text product strings have no such ceiling: each new submitter spells the same proteins a new way. Sampling 153 annotated genomes 100 times over shows the two curves doing exactly that — the module is saturated after one genome, while the source vocabulary is still climbing at 153.

13strings, controlled vocabulary
25strings, BV-BRC free text
1.9×collapse per 100 genomes
93genomes before free text saturates

The ratio is the number worth quoting: at the end of each curve, free text costs 16.3 strings per 100 genomes and the controlled vocabulary 8.5 — a 1.9-fold collapse. Reaching 95% of its final size takes the source vocabulary 93 genomes (61% of the set); the module gets there on genome 1.

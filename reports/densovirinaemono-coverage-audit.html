01What the module covers

One module for the four monosense densovirus genera, whose coding strand carries both the nonstructural and the structural genes.

The module covers Iteradensovirus, Brevidensovirus, Hepandensovirus and Muscodensovirus. These are the Densovirinae whose genes all read from one strand, which is what separates them from the ambisense genera held in the sister module. The layout is the same across the four: a nonstructural cassette at the 5′ end and a nested capsid set at the 3′ end.

Four features are declared: NS1, NS2, VP1 and VP2. Only NS1 carries copy_num, so only NS1 is treated as essential when a genome is scored for quality. VP1 and VP2 are nested alternative-start forms of one capsid gene and are exempt from the consolidation rule that would otherwise merge them.

Thirty-one distinct annotation strings collapse onto the four feature names. NS1 draws on nine of them — nonstructural protein 1, non-structural protein 1, nonstructural protein NS1, NS1, helicase NS1 and four more — and VP1 on eight. The collapse is what makes a profile possible at all: a cluster built from one spelling would be built from a fraction of the available sequence.

Three sequences did not collapse and are not modelled: two putative nonstructural protein NS1 and two viral protein in Iteradensovirus, and one non-structure protein 2 in Brevidensovirus. They are recorded in UNASSIGNED_TRACKING.tsv. That file is a list of unfinished work, not a resolution — these five sequences did leave the module.

04Does it route?

Routing is a BLASTn of the query against the reference contigs, so a genome with no close relative among them is invisible to the module.

Routing was measured against the 11 reference contigs this module ships, of which 11 were used by the exemplar set.

Eleven reference contigs stand for the four genera. All twenty-four exemplar genomes routed, and all twenty-four routed to this module rather than to a neighbouring one. Nothing was unrouted.

That result is bounded by the sample, not a guarantee. Routing depends on nucleotide identity to a reference, and a divergent isolate with no close relative in the eleven will not route no matter how recognisable its proteins are.

07What it cannot do

Three limits, each measured rather than supposed.

No splice features are declared. The monosense genera express their nonstructural proteins from the one coding strand, and nothing in the collections showed the two-exon signature that put NS2 and NS3 into the splice caller in Amdoparvovirus and Protoparvovirus. If a spliced product exists here, this module does not call it and would not report its absence.

Only NS1 is essential. A genome missing its capsid entirely still scores clean. That is deliberate — VP1 and VP2 are called in 16 and 18 of 24 exemplars, well under the 0.85 that would justify copy_num — but it means the quality score is a weaker statement here than in a module where the capsid is required.

The module is bounded by what BV-BRC annotated. A protein these genera encode but that nobody deposited under any of the thirty-one strings is not a gap in this page; it is absent from it.

Use this module for monosense densovirus genomes that route. Do not read the forty-of-forty panel figure as an out-of-sample result, and do not read a clean score as evidence that the capsid was called.

08What the run found

What the build turned up that was not expected.

The capsid names in the deposits are unreliable. Within VP2's thirteen source strings sit major capsid protein VP3, structural protein VP3 and structural protein VP4 — three different nested forms deposited under one slot. The deposited name is a claim about which form a submitter thought they had, not a measurement, and the profiles were rebuilt after binning the nested forms on size rather than on name.

After that rebuild, the number of genomes in which two capsid features were called at identical coordinates fell from 16 of 40 to 0 of 40.

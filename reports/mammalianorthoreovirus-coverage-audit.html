01What the module covers

One genus split four ways, and this is the human-relevant quarter.

Mammalian orthoreovirus infects humans and other mammals and is rarely pathogenic, but it is a long-standing model system for dsRNA virus replication and is under active development as an oncolytic agent. The genome is ten dsRNA segments encoding eleven proteins:

L1  lambda-3   1,267 aa   RNA-dependent RNA polymerase
L2  lambda-2   1,289 aa   capping enzyme / core turret
L3  lambda-1   1,275 aa   core shell
M1  mu-2         736 aa   minor core protein, polymerase cofactor
M2  mu-1         708 aa   major outer capsid
M3  mu-NS        721 aa   non-structural, viral factories
S1  sigma-1      455 aa   cell attachment   + sigma-1s ~115 aa  (BICISTRONIC)
S2  sigma-2      418 aa   core
S3  sigma-NS     366 aa   non-structural
S4  sigma-3      365 aa   major outer capsid
Orthoreovirus is four modules, not one, and three independent lines agree: S1 cistron count, vocabulary and sequence. Best blastp identity on the core shell is 43.7% avian to mammalian, 32.3% mammalian to piscine and 29.5% mammalian to Pteropine, against 95–100% within each group. The Pteropine result was the unexpected one — they are bat viruses and read as mammalian on host, but they carry the avian S1 architecture (p10, p17, sigma C) and were excluded from this module on that basis.

02How it was built

A segmented taxon stored one segment per record, and a vocabulary where length is more reliable than any name.

BV-BRC stores one genome record per segment here. The family's 19,948 "genomes" are 0.5–4 kb, and a --min-len 15000 contig fetch returns zero. Every budget, completeness and panel decision in this module had to be written in those terms, and the panel had to be reassembled from segments before it could test anything.

MRV is deposited under 255 distinct strings for 11 proteins, and the binning rules are written around the fact that length is far more diagnostic than any string. Every protein has a tight modal length — most vary by under 1% — so the windows sit close. The bare segment labels are sent to the ambiguous pile rather than bound: L1 is deposited in this dump on proteins of 455 and 1,267 residues, so the label says nothing on its own.

Segment lengths were measured, not assumed. Each feature's collection names its source records, each record is a segment, so the segment's length distribution falls out directly: L segments 3.9 kb, M 2.2 kb, S 1.2–1.4 kb, which is where MRV biology puts them.

Self-recall against each feature's own in-range collection is 100% on all eleven. Cross-feature contamination: 1,322 sequences against 31 cluster references, nothing above 50 bits — which matters here because eleven features of one virus are being told apart, not eleven unrelated proteins.

Call positions were checked rather than assumed: the initiating Met is recovered on 87–100% of calls per feature, 100% for mu-1 and sigma-1s. The remainder are segment records that are incomplete at their 5′ end, where the scan to Met reaches the end of the record instead.

06Are the calls right?

The Met-start gate passed first time, which has not happened often.

Every CDS profile begins at its protein's start, reported by check_met_start.py on the first run over all 31 alignments. Norovirus, Sapovirus and Mamastrovirus each needed a round of N-terminal repair before they could say that; this module did not. The tight length distributions are the likely reason — a protein deposited at 1,275 residues by nearly everyone leaves the aligner nothing ragged to trim.

No feature is derived by cutting a precursor, so there is no cleavage boundary to get wrong. The only structural subtlety is the bicistronic S1, and sigma-1 and sigma-1s are declared as separate CDSs because each initiates at its own Met.

07What it cannot do

Three limits, all of them named rather than implied.

32 clusters holding 38 segment records do not route within the 33-reference budget. A long tail of divergent singletons is a documented gap rather than a defect, but it is stated here.

sigma-1s rests on 21 sequences. It is deposited far less often than the protein whose frame it overlaps — 25 unique records against 228 for sigma-1 — so its four profiles are built from the thinnest collection in the module. It is called on all 30 panel isolates, so the thinness has not yet cost anything measurable, but it is the feature most likely to miss a divergent serotype.

This module covers one quarter of its genus. Avian (6,733 records), Piscine (4,680) and Pteropine (294) orthoreoviruses are real populations that this module deliberately does not call, and none of them is built yet. A genome from any of them will route here only if no better module exists, and will then be annotated poorly or not at all.

08What the run found

No new kit defects. The surprise was taxonomic.

Pteropine and Nelson Bay orthoreoviruses are not mammalian orthoreoviruses in any sense this project cares about. They were caught because p17 and membrane fusion protein — avian S1 proteins — appeared in a subset that should have contained neither. All 45 carriers turned out to be Pteropine or Nelson Bay, and measuring them against MRV gave 29.5% identity on the core shell and 30.7% on sigma-2. They are now a separate group of 294 records awaiting their own module.

A second, smaller correction: the vocabulary rarefaction first reported this module emitting 11 strings against only 32 source proteins, which would have made the collapse look like 1.3×. The cause was the reconstruction — a panel isolate is keyed by one of its ten segment records, so only that segment's source annotations matched. Aggregating the source annotations across each isolate's own segments gives the real comparison: 104 source strings against 11.

09What the vocabulary bought

Eleven proteins, 221 ways of saying so.

221 distinct annotation strings across 2,594 occurrences collapse onto 11 controlled annotations — a 20.1× reduction. The polymerase alone arrives as lambda-3, lambda 3, lambda3, lambda-3 protein, lambda 3 RNA polymerase, RNA-dependent RNA polymerase and RNA-directed RNA polymerase (EC 2.7.7.48).

Rarefied over proteins and compared at the depth both curves reach: the source vocabulary holds 104 distinct strings and the module holds 11, a 9.5× collapse. The source needs 264 proteins to reach 95% of its final vocabulary; the module needs 30, which is one protein per feature plus slack.

Does the hepatitis E module actually work?

Hepeviridae is the only family in class Alsuviricetes that infects humans: Paslahepevirus balayani is hepatitis E virus, around 20 million infections a year. This is the audit of the module built for it — what it covers, where its thresholds sit, whether genomes reach it, whether it calls the proteins, and what it cannot do. 01What the module covers

One module for the whole family, and that was measured rather than assumed.

Every near-complete genome in BV-BRC was bucketed by ORF architecture from its own CDS coordinates. Every genus came out the same shape — ORF1 nonstructural polyprotein, ORF2 capsid, ORF3 viroporin — with Rocahepevirus adding a fourth small ORF. So one module is right, with four features.

The family is dominated by one species to a degree that shapes everything downstream: 25,438 of 29,294 records are hepatitis E virus, and the median record in the family is 347 nucleotides. Only 1,574 reach 6 kb.

02How it was built

12,334 distinct protein sequences, triaged by text rule, then by homology, then by position.

stepeffect
text rules3,331 in range, 8,406 length-outliers, 523 matched no rule
homology rescue443 of 523 adopted at ≥80% identity over ≥60% of the query; 78 deliberately left out
outlier re-route26 moved to a different feature (ORF2→ORF3 14, ORF3→ORF2 8, ORF1→ORF3 3, ORF2→ORF1 1)
final collectionsORF1 1,429 · ORF2 1,270 · ORF3 974 · ORF4 45
profiles21 · 15 · 23 · 4 = 63 PSSMs

The re-route step exists because qc_cross_feature.py flagged four mislabels that were all real: 119–123 aa proteins annotated “nonstructural protein” or “ORF2” that are actually ORF3. The length gate had correctly parked them as outliers — but under the wrong feature. After re-routing, the QC reports no contamination.

One alignment was retired: ORF3 cluster 8, 75 aa, 100% identical to residues 25–99 of a 122-aa ORF3. A pure internal fragment. The pipeline's own truncation QC missed it because it tests containment in one direction only.

03Where the cutoffs sit

ORF3, on the 42-genome held-out panel:

  noise ceiling              78.6   a SECOND HSP of the chosen profile,
                                    query residues 1-64, in ORF2's frame
  typical cross-frame noise  26-35  present in 29 of 42 genomes
  weakest genuine call       99.0

  -> any cutoff in 79..99 works; 90 chosen, erring toward specificity

That single spurious HSP, extended outward by the annotator, grew to ORF2's exact span and was the only duplicate call in the whole evaluation. Raising the cutoff to 90 removed it.

The cost is recorded, not hidden. ORF3 is no longer called on three Chirohepevirus genomes that scored 62.6, 77.2 and 84.9. That is a coverage hole, not a threshold problem — see §07.

Routing improved 31 → 36 → 42 on the held-out panel as references were added. Six of those 24 rep contigs are the only near-complete genome of their species in BV-BRC, so they reference themselves; they give production coverage but cannot count as validation, and are listed in Rep-Contigs/SELF_REFERENCING.tsv. The honest held-out figure is 36 of 42.

The remaining gap was deliberately not closed. minimal_refs.py returns a perfectly flat curve — every additional reference buys exactly one genome, because all 47 unrouted exemplars are singleton clusters. Closing it fully means going from 24 to 71 rep contigs to recover 7% of exemplars, and 46 of the 47 are one-off metagenomic hepe-like viruses: Barns Ness breadcrumb sponge hepe-like virus 1, 2 and 3, Wenling fish hepevirus, Dongbei arctic lamprey hepevirus. You cannot reference your way out of a long tail.

The 81% is not a coverage rate: evaluate_module.py matches one-to-one by call index and cannot match a mature peptide to a reference CDS. The numbers that matter are 0 duplicates and the misses, which all fall in the two genera named in §07.

No feature in this module is non-collinear. The step 6b test was run on one reference genome per genus: every ORF1, ORF2 and ORF3 returns exactly one tblastn HSP in a single reading frame. No readthrough, no frameshift, no cleaved products, so no mature peptides and no SignalP.

ORF4 is two different proteins, and only one is declared. Paslahepevirus ORF4 and Rocahepevirus ORF4 share a positional label and nothing else: over every ORF4-annotated sequence in the dump, the best cross-genus BLASTp alignment is 5 residues at e-value 138. The rule is therefore gated on homology to a verified seed rather than on genus — which matters, because an audit of every ORF1 against every other found 16 of 1,417 genomes whose BV-BRC genus label contradicts its own sequence, rat and ferret HEV deposited twice under two taxon ids at 96–99% identity.

ORF1 is declared whole, and the seven RefSeq domains are not. That annotation descends from Koonin 1992 (PMID 1518855), titled “Computer-assisted assignment of functional domains” and describing them as putative. Perttilä 2013 (PMID 23255617) found no processing across several expression systems with domain-specific antisera; LeDesma 2023 (PMID 36852909) found the “protease” cysteines coordinate metal ions structurally. Declaring seven mature peptides would assert seven cleavages the field has not agreed happen. All three citations are model-proposed and carry the PMID_claude_generated flag.

The skill warns that “feature is too short” dominating means the length bounds are wrong rather than the genomes. So the flagged calls were measured: ORF1 at 474–1,222 aa against a full length of ~1,700, ORF2 at 129–487 against ~660. They are genuinely fragments. And 20 of the 26 genomes carrying a short feature are already flagged for ambiguous bases — N-runs cropping the call. Only 6 are short without that explanation.

At most ~14 of 622 genomes (about 2%) are poor for reasons the module could fix.

09What the vocabulary bought

The point of LowVan is a controlled vocabulary. This is what it cost the free text.

A controlled vocabulary saturates, because the nth genome reuses names the first n−1 established. Free text climbs, because every submitter spells the same protein a new way. Free text reaches 95% of its final vocabulary only at 533 genomes and is still climbing; the controlled set gets there at 13. At the end of each curve: 10.2 strings per 100 genomes against 0.6 — a 15.8-fold difference.

The typo cluster is the concrete evidence of what the source looks like: caspid protein, capsid protein 2, hypothetical protein 1/2/3.

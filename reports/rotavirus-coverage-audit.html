One module for all ten rotavirus species. Eleven double-stranded RNA segments, six structural proteins and six non-structural ones, and an annotation vocabulary in which 38% of the deposited proteins declare themselves to be fragments. This audit says what the module calls, what it does not call, and which of those gaps are biology and which are ours.

01What the module covers

Rotavirus is one genus, and that is the whole module. The partitioning rule asks about genome organisation rather than phylogeny, and every rotavirus species carries the same eleven segments in the same arrangement, so the ten species stay together.

Rotaviruses are the leading cause of severe childhood gastroenteritis worldwide. Species A accounts for essentially all human disease and for 89% of the genus dump; species B, C and H cause human outbreaks at much lower frequency, and D through G, I and J are animal viruses. That imbalance is the central technical problem in this module and is visible in almost every number below.

Every record in this family's BV-BRC dump is a single segment. Nothing in Sedoreoviridae arrives as an assembled multi-segment genome the way influenza does, so the routing and coverage measurements below are made on reassembled genomes: records sharing a strain name and contributing exactly eleven segments were merged, which recovers 6,627 genomes with a contig set of at least 5 kb.

The species count is the ICTV set, Rotavirus A through Rotavirus J, all ten of which are present in the dump. A further 9,577 genomes carry a name from which no species letter can be read and are not counted here.

The twelve protein collections were binned by rule from the source annotation and then widened by homology rescue, which adopted 3,279 features under 186 further strings — mostly bare forms such as VP4 and inner capsid protein VP6 that no rule could safely claim on text alone.

VP8* and VP5* have no collection in the dump, because nothing in BV-BRC is annotated as a mature rotavirus spike subunit. They are a coordinate operation on the curated VP4: trypsin cleaves VP4 after Arg247, and the site was located as the motif R↓AQ in a 230–265 window rather than as a column number, so it re-locates itself after any rebuild (Gilbert & Greenberg 1998, J Virol 72:5323–5327).

That motif is a species A observation and it behaves like one. It finds a unique site in 88% of species A VP4 and in essentially none of any other species: 1 of 271 in species C, 0 of 60 in species B, 0 of 53 in species H. Species A VP4 is 33% identical to species C VP4 and 19% identical to species B, far below the 80% floor this project requires before a cleavage site may be transferred across an alignment, so the site was not projected onto them. The consequence is a real coverage gap and it is reported as one in §05 and §07, not papered over.

Every length window in this module is explicit. The usual test — a window taken as a fraction of the feature's pooled median — cannot work here, because species A is the pooled median. A 0.7–1.3× window from it deletes 61% of non-A NSP3, 57% of NSP1 and 35% of VP7. Each window instead spans the range of per-species full lengths, and each of those was estimated from cleanly-labelled records only.

04Does it route?

Routing is the step this family was expected to fail, and it did not.

33 rep contigs ship with the module, each one a whole 11-segment genome; 33 of them are the best BLASTn hit for at least one exemplar. Routing was measured against the Sedoreoviridae modules only — nucleotide BLASTn cannot reach the routing threshold across virus families. Every incoming record in this family is a single segment, so a segment routes if it resembles any segment of any chosen reference; that is why the segment-level budget in §04 is the one that predicts runtime behaviour and the concatenated-genome budget is not.

An earlier budget run suggested the opposite. Clustering each genome's eleven concatenated segments gave 1,574 clusters for 6,648 genomes, 74% of them singletons, and a projected reach of 49% for species A and 0% for everything else. That figure was an artefact of the method: rotaviruses reassort, so a concatenated genome is a mosaic and every new combination of otherwise-common segments becomes its own cluster.

What actually happens at runtime is different. Every incoming record is one segment, a rep contig is a whole genome carrying all eleven segment types, and a segment routes if it resembles any segment of any chosen reference. Clustering segments and choosing genomes greedily by uncovered segment clusters reaches 100% at the project budget. The same flaw is present in the shipped repcontig_budget.py, which concatenates per genome, and it very likely understates the influenza budgets measured with it.

The reference set was raised from 25 to 33 to close a routing gap in the minor species. Splitting the module was considered and rejected: the species differ in sequence, not in genome organisation, and a split would have produced nine modules that each declare the same fourteen features.

232 of 440 routed exemplars get every one of the 13 core features. Features trailing the best-called core protein by 20 points or more: VP8*, VP5*. Excluded from that count: NSP6 — called by an external program and absent from this table, or a protein part of the genus does not encode.

NSP6 is excluded from the core set because its absence is biology rather than coverage. It is an out-of-frame ORF on segment 11, overlapping NSP5, and it is a species A feature: 282 of 297 species A exemplars get it and zero of the 115 exemplars carrying any other species letter do. A profile set that called NSP6 in species B or C would be wrong.

VP8* and VP5* are a different matter and are deliberately left in the core count so the gap shows. They are called in 291 of 297 species A exemplars and in none of species B, C, D, F, G, H or I — while VP4 itself is called in 431 of 440. Every rotavirus cleaves VP4; this module can only locate the cut in one species. The honest reading of §05 is that the module calls the rotavirus proteome in species A and the rotavirus proteome minus two mature peptides everywhere else.

Read the headline count with that in mind. 373 of 440 routed exemplars (84.8%) get every one of the eleven essential CDS features — the eleven the genome quality tool actually requires. The lower figure above is the same measurement with VP8* and VP5* added back in, and the difference between the two is the species A restriction on the cleavage site, not a second problem.

VP2 trails the other structural proteins at 91%. Six records of species A VP4 length had been binned into VP2 from a conflicting annotation string and were moved to the outliers file; the remaining shortfall is in the unlabelled genomes and has not been chased.

The panel was drawn round-robin across species so that the minor species are represented far above their share of the dump: five genomes each from species A, B, C, G, H and I, three from D, two from F and five unlabelled. Every genome used as a rep contig was excluded. This makes the panel a much harder test than the dump's composition would produce, and the flag tally should be read with that in mind.

Every missing-essential flag pairs with a missing contig. Three genomes are flagged Contig is missing: NSP5 Segment and the same three for a missing NSP5; two for the VP4 Segment and two for VP4; two for the NSP3 Segment and two for NSP3; one for the VP1 Segment and one for the polymerase. Naming segments after the protein they carry is what makes that pairing readable at a glance. The module is not failing to call those proteins — the deposited genome does not contain the segment that encodes them. The other 30 flags are Contig is too short (22) and too many ambiguous bases (8), both properties of the deposit.

By species: all six species A genomes are clean, all five species B and all five species C. Every flagged genome is species D, F, G, H, I or unlabelled — the thinly-sequenced animal rotaviruses, whose deposits are the shortest and most ambiguous in the family. A panel weighted to the dump's own composition would be ~90% species A and would have reported a much higher clean rate while testing much less.

07What it cannot do

What this module cannot do.

The spike subunits are species A only. VP8* and VP5* will not be called on species B, C, D, F, G, H, I or J. The fix is a documented or directly determined cleavage site for at least species B and C VP4; until one exists, transferring the species A site across a 19–33% identity alignment would be a guess, and this project does not make that kind of guess silently.

NSP6 is modelled for species A alone, which is correct as far as the literature goes, but the literature on segment 11 in the minor species is thin. If an NSP6-equivalent ORF is later described in species B or C, this module will not call it.

Nine of the ten species are represented by tens of genomes, not thousands. Species E contributes 5 genomes to the whole dump and species J contributes 70. The profiles for those species rest on very few independent observations, and the self-recall figure in §03 cannot distinguish a profile that generalises from one that has memorised its four training sequences.

Reassortment is not modelled. The module annotates segments independently and makes no statement about whether a given set of eleven segments constitutes a coherent strain.

The module is complete for Rotavirus A, which is the human pathogen, and complete apart from the two spike subunits for the other nine species. Both gaps are named above and neither is hidden inside a number.

08What the run found

What the run turned up that was not expected.

38% of the dump declares itself incomplete. 66,897 of 173,758 protein features carry a source annotation ending in , fragment, , N-terminal, , C-terminal or beginning truncated, and under this project's rule all of them are discarded rather than binned. VP4 and VP7 account for most of it, which is unsurprising — those are the genotyping targets and are routinely sequenced in part.

That discard changed a consensus. Before it, the VP7 collection held 10,105 truncated species A records against 5,461 full-length ones, and the species A median read 311 residues against a true 326. The truncated records outnumbered the real ones two to one, so they set their own window and passed it. Length alone would not have caught this; the source label is what separated the two populations, and estimating full length from cleanly-labelled records only is what made the estimate unbiased.

A single string pattern bound 221 features to the wrong protein. Deposits of the form outer capsid glycoprotein VP6, outer capsid glycoprotein NSP4 and seven other variants matched both the VP7 rule and the rule for the named gene. Under a first-match binning order they all became VP7. The collection engine now refuses any string that two rules claim and routes it to the homology rescue instead.

Putative is not partial. The two qualifiers were conflated in an early pass. putative expresses uncertainty about identity and those records are kept and arbitrated by homology; truncated expresses incompleteness and is always discarded. Several of the largest discarded strings carry both, for example putative outer capsid spike protein VP4, fragment, and are discarded on the second qualifier.

Three features were demanding to exist. The genome quality tool treats a feature as essential if it declares copy_num and is a CDS or mature peptide, and all fourteen declared it. So every non-species-A genome was flagged poor for lacking VP8*, VP5* and NSP6 — proteins this audit says it does not expect them to have. The first panel run scored 6 of 40 clean for that reason alone. Dropping copy_num from those three took it to 22 of 40 and left behind only flags that name a real property of the deposit. The picornavirus modules had the convention right already: the parent CDS is essential, its mature peptides are not.

A segmented reference was shipping as one fused contig. build_rep_contigs.py joined every record in a genome's file and wrote a single contig, so each rotavirus reference was 18 kb of sequence under its own first segment's header. Routing was unaffected — re-measuring 440 exemplars against the before and after files returns the identical set — which is exactly why it survived: nothing failed and nothing said so. The files now carry one record per segment, as the influenza references always have.

The rarefaction curve is the stronger statement because it controls for sampling depth.

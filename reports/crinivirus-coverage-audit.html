Does the cucurbit yellow stunting disorder virus module actually work?

Crinivirus is the bipartite genus of the Closteroviridae: cucurbit yellow stunting disorder virus, tomato chlorosis virus, sweet potato chlorotic stunt virus and about thirty more, all whitefly-transmitted and phloem-limited. RNA1 carries a ~1,990 aa replicase polyprotein and the RdRp; RNA2 carries the closterovirid quintuple gene block. This module shipped only after a duplicate-call audit found four pairs of features that were one protein each.

01What the module covers

Bipartite, so one genome arrives as two records and must be merged before annotation.

Every BV-BRC record carries contigs=1, so a crinivirus genome is deposited as two separate records sharing a name. merge_segments.py is mandatory at annotation time, and it correctly refuses to merge a name whose record count exceeds the segment count — 45 name collisions in this taxon, several isolates deposited under one name.

Segment assignment was measured, not assumed: anchoring RNA1 on the records carrying POLY/RDRP and RNA2 on those carrying HSP70/CPM gives two sets with zero overlap, and every feature falls entirely on one side. A discovered cluster that straddled both would have been an artefact.

02How it was built

1,749 unique proteins, and the accessory names are worthless.

A mass is not a homology group. Measured by clustering the mass-named pool: p22 is four non-homologous groups at 188, 191, 192 and 193 aa; p59 and p60 are one group of 78 that is the HSP90 homolog; p6/p7/p8/p9 fragment into at least seven groups by size. Accessory features are therefore discovered by clustering and only then named, with A/B/C suffixes where one name covers several groups.

The 13 records labelled movement protein are all exactly 518 aa and 98–99% identical to the p59/p60 collection. In closterovirids the HSP90 homolog is part of the movement machinery, which is what that label reports; they were binned to HSP90 rather than given a feature that would have duplicated it.

The ORF1a/1b fusion — 46 records at 2,413–2,499 aa — is the +1 frameshift readthrough product and is not declared. ORF1a and the RdRp are called separately, as in the shipped Closterovirus module.

repcontig_budget.py --merge-by-name reported 100.0%. The shipped set covers 511 of 553 records = 92.4%. The gate had merged records by genome name, but 18 names carry between 3 and 8 records — isolate collisions rather than segments — so several genomes were counted as one. Same defect that took Bromoviridae from 94.8% to 69.5%.

92.4% is the honest figure. An estimate above the run is always a bug; an estimate below it is merely conservative.

Before the audit in §08, eleven features cleared the 85% threshold and five of those were counting calls made inside another feature's coordinates. After it, eight clear it and all eight are supported.

P22A's 0.0% is not a broken profile. All 26 of its source records sit on genome records under 6 kb, and the evaluation population is genomes at or above 6 kb, so the feature was never offered a genome that could carry it. It recovers 26 of its own 26 sequences. A low call rate has two causes that look identical in the report — the protein is rare, or its genomes are partial — and one query on the source records' lengths separates them.

07What it cannot do

Three limits, one of them permanent.

HSP70 and HSP90 cannot be separated on 6% of genomes. They are called on identical coordinates there. This is not a binning error — the two collections share no sequences at all — but genuine paralogy: in criniviruses the two proteins are 64% identical over 99% of their length, and no bit_cutoff separates them without losing one. A documented limit, not a defect to fix.

The evaluation excludes partial genomes. Collections are drawn from every protein record in the dump; the evaluation only from genomes at or above 6 kb. Features whose sequences come mostly from partial records — P22A entirely, P7A and RNASE3 substantially — show call rates that understate their real prevalence.

No mature peptides. ORF1a is a polyprotein and its cleavage sites are not documented for this genus in a form that can be located from the sequence.

08What the run found

Eight features that were counting another protein's locus as their own.

The module originally declared 23 features. A duplicate-call audit of the scored genomes found four pairs or groups that were one protein each, plus one short profile firing inside a longer feature.

RNASE3 + SUPPRESSOR   99% id / 100% cov  -> one gene, merged
p26 + p27 + p28       23-24% id / 96-99% cov -> one family, merged into P27
p8  + p9              33-34% id / 91-99% cov -> one family, merged into P8A
p6  + p8B             29-36% id / 79-81% cov -> one family, merged into P8B
P23 inside P22B 82%   29% id / 35% cov       -> promiscuous, dropped
HSP70 + HSP90, 6%     64% id / 99% cov       -> real paralogues, kept

In Sweet potato chlorotic stunt virus the RNase3 is the silencing suppressor, so RNASE3 and SUPPRESSOR were one gene declared twice. The three mass-named groups are the mass trap again — this time splitting one protein into several rather than merging several into one.

The defect raises a feature's call rate, which is why nothing else caught it. A profile firing inside a longer feature is present on nearly every genome that has the longer one, so it looks essential. Self-recall did not see it: every profile recovered its own collection. Collection disjointness did not see it: blastp at 90% identity found zero containment.

The first version of the check was itself wrong, and that is worth recording. It counted any overlap with a longer feature as nesting, with no containment floor — and adjacent genes in a compact viral genome overlap routinely. On that faulty measure two real features (P8A and P8B) were dropped before the error was caught; P8A is called on 100% of genomes. The check now requires the shorter feature to be 80% contained.

Resolved: 23 features to 17, quality 81.3% to 90.0%, eight honestly-essential features.

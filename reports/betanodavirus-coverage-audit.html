Does the fish nervous necrosis virus module actually work?

The largest module of the four, and the one whose headline fact is a negative. Unlike the insect nodaviruses, the betanodavirus capsid is not cleaved: its RNA2 ORF “carries the catalytic D-75 residue but lacks the site for autocatalytic proteolysis” (PMID 9672600). No beta and no gamma are declared here, and proving that absence is part of what this audit reports.

01What the module covers

Four proteins on two segments, and nothing else is claimed.

The fish nervous necrosis viruses — SJNNV, RGNNV, BFNNV and TPNNV, plus the Atlantic cod and greasy grouper isolates inside them. 1,456 genomes, by far the most-sequenced group in the family. RNA1 carries the replicase and, from a subgenomic RNA3, the non-structural proteins B1 and B2; RNA2 carries a single uncleaved coat protein.

The capsid is not cleaved, and that is why this is a separate module from Alphanodavirus. Both genera carry the same four gene slots, so on a gene count they look identical. PMID 9672600 measured the difference directly and also excluded alternative initiation, a −1 frameshift and precursor degradation as explanations for the observed 43/45 kDa doublet. The T=3 crystal structure is built from the intact 338-residue chain (PMID 26491970), and the 30-year review states it in terms (PMID 32050492). A module holding both genera would have to declare beta and gamma for 1,456 genomes that never make them.

The N-ARM, S domain and P domain described in that structure are regions of one continuous chain, not products, and are deliberately not modelled as mature peptides.

Subgenomic RNA3 is made by premature termination, not by splicing (PMID 16186236; PMID 41146288 for the intragenomic RNA–RNA pairing mechanism). This module therefore declares no spliced feature, and that is a positive statement about the biology rather than an omission.

02How it was built

332 training sequences, and every length window taken from the literature because the deposits cannot carry one.

This genus is dominated by partial typing deposits, and that single fact determines how every window here was set. The median coat protein record is 181 aa against a true 338, and the median polymerase record is 300 against 982. A window anchored on any deposited median would delete every full-length protein in the genus and keep the fragments.

B1 had to be built without a published start codon. PMID 19136133 cloned the RGNNV B1 gene as 336 nt and showed the protein is a real anti-necrotic death factor, and PMID 36560822 confirms its reading frame “matches the 3′ termini of the RdRp reading frame” — but no paper gives a start coordinate and no RefSeq annotates a B1 CDS anywhere in the genus. BV-BRC holds 2 deposits across 1,456 genomes. The 115 members were extracted from the genomes under the one property the literature does fix:

  B1 is in frame with protein A and shares its stop codon, so an accepted
  member must be an EXACT C-terminal substring of protein A from its OWN genome.

The structural gate is what makes a 2-deposit feature buildable. Identity alone could not do it: B1 is 100% identical to protein A over its own length by construction, so an identity test cannot distinguish a correct B1 from a fragment cut anywhere else in the replicase.

03Where the cutoffs sit

Raised from measured panel scores, and re-measured afterwards.

The generated cutoff is a floor, not a threshold. In Tobaniviridae a floor of that kind let a spurious call land at 30.34 bits while the genuine protein on the same genome scored 476.8 — and neither self-recall nor a held-out panel can see that, because both test sequences that really are the protein. Each cutoff here was set to half the lowest score among the feature's 80 genuine panel calls, capped at 500.

The cap matters as much as the rule. Uncapped, protein A's cutoff would have been 927 — and an uncapped cutoff drawn from a long, highly conserved protein excludes exactly the divergent genomes a profile exists to find.

Re-measured after the raise: every feature still calls on 100% of routed genomes and all 80 panel genomes are still graded good. No genuine call was lost.

Three references suffice because this genus is tight: 603 genomes collapse to six clusters at 70% nucleotide identity, and the largest two hold most of them. Both segments are represented — a 3,112 nt RNA1 and a 1,739 nt RNA2 — which matters because BV-BRC stores each segment as its own record, so an orphan RNA2 deposit has to find an RNA2 reference.

Measured at the annotator's own BLASTn settings rather than with check_rep_contigs.py, which calls bare blastn — megablast, which finds little below about 85% identity and would under-report the divergent isolates.

On complete genomes all four features are called every time. Across all 286 genomes that carry any call the picture is different and worth stating plainly: CP lands at 90% and the three RNA1 proteins at 42%. That gap is not a profile gap — it is the deposit profile of this genus. Most records are RNA2 capsid fragments submitted for genotyping, and a genome holding only RNA2 cannot call a protein encoded on RNA1.

A further 264 records route and call nothing. These are capsid fragments short enough to fall below the profile's coverage requirement; the annotator records the module match so the taxon is not lost, which is the designed behaviour for a partial deposit rather than a failure.

Every nesting the duplicate-call check reports is declared architecture: B1 inside protein A (an in-frame C-terminal fragment) and B2 inside protein A (a +1 overlapping gene). There are no exact duplicates and no undeclared nesting.

qc_cross_feature.py reports B1 as colliding with protein A at 100% identity. That is correct and expected — B1 is the C-terminus of protein A — and the tool has no way to be told so. The rows stand in the record and are answered here rather than suppressed; audit_bins.py, which does accept a declaration, reads Unresolved: 0 with the pair declared.

The one place a wrong answer would hide. B2 is marked essential despite sitting inside protein A, because every one of its calls is out of frame with the feature containing it — it is a separate gene, not a cleavage product. B1 is marked essential on the strength of the substring gate, not on its call rate alone.

07What it cannot do

Two genomes, and one whole population, that this module does not reach.

1. Two exemplars route and then call nothing, and more references will not help. 1168598.3 (Betanodavirus S1.Tun03.11) and 1110530.25 (Sparus aurata betanodavirus) hit the references at 951 and 761 bits, far above the 150-bit floor, and then return stop-free 0 for every profile — the annotator finds no stop-free ORF to match. Both are partial gene deposits whose reading frames are broken relative to the profiles. This is a property of those records, not a threshold that can be lowered.

2. 264 records route and call nothing by design. They are capsid typing fragments below the coverage requirement. Reporting them as coverage failures would misdescribe the module; reporting them not at all would misdescribe the data. They are counted in §05 and left as they are.

3. 224 Betanodavirus sp. records were dropped at intake and are outside the module's scope. The cost was measured before the decision rather than assumed: those 223 proteins are 97.6% mean identity to a named betanodavirus, so dropping them removes no diversity. It is a scope rule — LowVan does not build modules for viruses with no assigned species — not a quality judgement, and it is the opposite of the Ambidensovirus sp. bin the skill documents, which held 27–38% identity between its own classes.

A near-miss worth recording. The sp. intake rule matches an organism name containing sp. — and in this genus that regex also matches Mullus sp. betanodavirus, where the sp. belongs to the fish host, not the virus. Nineteen records across six host genera (Mullus, Gobius, Epinephelus, Pagellus, Balistapus, Pseudorhombus) are named viruses that the unqualified test would have deleted. The test is applied only to the part of the name before the virus word.

All four features carry copy_num, set from the measurement rather than from the gene's reputation: each is called on 100% of the 80 complete held-out genomes, and no complete genome fails to call any of them.

Ten genomes were folded into this module by measurement. Atlantic cod nodavirus carries no genus in BV-BRC and is 88.6% identical to this module's canonical polymerase over its full length, against 50–61% for every other species in the untabled tail. It is the BFNNV genotype, and BV-BRC already files the near-identical Atlantic cod betanodavirus records under genus Betanodavirus — an inconsistent assignment on one species, not a new lineage.

Two records were removed from this genus for the opposite reason. BV-BRC files Wuhan nodavirus and Mosquito nodavirus MNV-1 under genus Betanodavirus; both are insect noda-like viruses. A rep contig for either would route an insect virus into the fish module, which has no profile that can call it — turning a visible gap into a silent one.

The polymerase alone arrives as RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, RNA dependent RNA polymerase, RNA dependent-RNA polymerase, RNA-directed RNA polymerase, RNA-dependent RNA-polymerase, RNA dependant RNA polymerase, RNA polymerase, RdRp, RdRp polymerase, protein A and protein A (RDRP) — eleven spellings of one gene, several of them single-character typos of each other.

Two strings are new to the LowVan vocabulary: Non-structural protein B1 and Capsid protein. B2 reuses RNA silencing suppressor protein, which this genus has earned — betanodavirus B2 sequesters duplex RNA and antagonises RNAi (PMIDs 16352533, 16809288). The sibling crustacean module deliberately does not use that string, because the same activity has never been demonstrated there.

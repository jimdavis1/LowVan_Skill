LowVan · Tobaniviridae · 18 November 2026

Four modules for a family that is four different genomes

Tobaniviridae was split out of Coronaviridae in 2018. Its four subfamilies share a replication strategy — one enormous replicase ORF reached by a −1 ribosomal frameshift, then a nested set of subgenomic mRNAs — and very little else: cattle and pig toroviruses carry a haemagglutinin-esterase, fish bafiniviruses carry nothing beyond S, M and N, the single bovine nidovirus carries two accessory ORFs of its own, and the snake and lizard serpentoviruses carry three more that resemble nothing. This is the audit of the four modules built for them: what they cover, how the cleavage sites and the frameshift junction were settled from the literature rather than from the records, whether genomes route, whether the proteins are called, and the several things these modules deliberately cannot do.

01What the modules cover

Four modules, split on genome organisation, with every genome assigned from sequence rather than from its BV-BRC label.

The 147 near-complete genomes were grouped by replicase-protein identity — blastx, HSP-length-weighted, single linkage at 40% — and every remaining genome took the module its proteins' best blastp hits agreed on. The grouping reproduces the ICTV subfamilies and the gene layouts, and it disagrees with the deposited genus labels in a way that matters: three genomes labelled Torovirus (the Guangdong snake “toroviruses”) group with Serpentovirinae at 76–100% replicase identity and carry the serpentovirus gene layout, as do 120 records filed only as Serpentovirinae sp.

Bafinivirus and Oncotshavirus share one module because they share the layout and their replicases are 96.7–98.8% identical. Everything else is separated by gene content, not by phylogeny: HE exists only in Torovirus, the bostovirus G2 and 88-residue ORFs exist nowhere else, and the serpentovirus accessories have no counterpart outside that subfamily.

Two singleton lineages are tabled and not shipped: Glycera tridactyla tobanivirus, whose nearest relative anywhere in the family is bovine nidovirus at 28.1% replicase identity, and Salmo salar tobanivirus at 42.4%, which has no slippery heptamer in phase with its ORF1a stop. One genome each, no close relative; a module built on one genome describes that genome.

Serpentovirinae could not be binned on its names and the attempt was measured, not assumed. ORF numbering is not portable across this family — serpentovirus ORF3 is an unknown accessory protein while bafinivirus and bostovirus ORF3 is M — and within the subfamily one protein arrives as hypothetical protein (×10), membrane protein (×6), putative membrane protein (×4), M protein (×2) and putative matrix protein, while minor M protein names a different protein altogether. A rule set over those strings put the real M, 13 unique sequences, into an accessory bag and left the M collection holding 4.

So that module bins by homology. All 138 deposited proteins under 2,500 aa are clustered at 25% identity, and each cluster is tested against the three proteins the literature names in NC_024709 (PMID 25205093): ORF2 = S, ORF4 = M, ORF5a = N. S hits at e = 0; M picks up two orthogroups (e = 1e-163 and e = 2e-17 over 163 aa); N picks up five (down to e = 3e-07 over 106 aa); every other cluster's best hit is e ≥ 0.01 over at most 72 aa, i.e. nothing. Genome position was read afterwards and agrees, but nothing is called by position — position only separates one orthogroup from another.

The nine clusters with no hit to S, M or N and at least two distinct sequences became UNC1–UNC9, all of them Uncharacterized lineage-specific protein: no function is published for any serpentovirus accessory ORF, and the describing paper says so. 72 deposits in clusters holding a single distinct sequence — one isolate, usually deposited twice as GenBank plus RefSeq — are recorded in NOT_MODELLED.tsv rather than given a profile.

03Where the cutoffs sit, and what rests on one sequence

Most of this family is sampled a handful of genomes deep, and the profile sets say so.

Remotovirinae7 (1 transcript_edit)105
Serpentovirinae14 (1 transcript_edit)8583

The documented member floor is -m 5 per cluster and the identity floor -mi 0.8. These subfamilies hold 6 to 17 unique sequences per protein spread over several genera, so at -m 5 most features build zero profiles. The member floor was lowered to 2 and recorded in each feature's BUILD_PARAMS; the identity floor was not lowered, because lowering it redefines what counts as the same protein. What the identity floor costs instead is clustering: where a feature's sequences are 25–60% identical, every one of them is its own cluster, and each gets a profile of its own built from the sequence duplicated into a two-row alignment. That is an honest model of one sequence, and the alternative was no profile at all.

A single-sequence profile is a lookup table with a bit score. It will call a close relative and it will not generalise; where a module's call rate is high on the genomes that resemble its references and lower elsewhere, this is the reason.

Each module ships only the references its own coverage needs rather than the full 25, and build_rep_contigs.py reproduces the budget figure exactly. The whole-taxon run confirms it from the other side: of 117 exemplars, 91 were annotated through the GTO pipeline and every one of them routed; the other 26 never reached a module because rast-create-genome returned HTTP 403 from the BV-BRC ID server, which the coverage report counts as UNROUTED. Re-run through the flat annotator, which needs no ID server, 24 of those 26 route as well. The two that do not are a 1,625 nt and a 1,316 nt fragment.

Routing is not this family's problem, at any level of divergence measured here: 115 of 117 distinct genome types reach a module, including the 120 metagenomic Serpentovirinae sp. records that are out of scope for the collections.

The accessory features are lineage-specific by construction and their rates are a property of the panel's composition rather than of the profiles: UNC1 57%, UNC2 50%, UNC3 43%, and UNC4–UNC9 7–14%, which is how often a panel genome belongs to the lineage that carries them. 06Are the calls right?

The mature peptide is the test that matters here, because it was cut at a published site rather than modelled.

Torovirinae Mpro, 24 panel calls      first residue  Ser 100%     last residue  Gln 100%
                                      length 287-307 aa   (literature: 292 EToV, 287 PToV)

Piscanivirinae Mpro, 13 panel calls   first residue  Ser 77%, Ala 15%, Leu 8%
                                      last residue   Gln 92%, Pro 8%
                                      length 260-275 aa   (literature: 267 WBV)

The torovirus main proteinase cleaves after Gln and leaves Ser at P1′ at both of its own boundaries — Gln3251↓Ser3252 and Gln3543↓Ser3544 in equine torovirus (PMID 16571831), Gln3095↓Ser3096 and Gln3382↓Ser3383 in porcine torovirus (PMID 32727876), all four mapped by Edman degradation of the cleavage products. Every one of the 24 calls reproduces that chemistry exactly. The bafinivirus protease uses alternative sites at both ends (PMID 21068254), which is why both Ser and Ala appear at P1′ there; one of the 13 calls drifts at both ends.

qc_cross_feature.py and audit_contamination.py are clean on all four modules — 492 sequences tested against each feature's canonical, no cross-feature contamination above 50 bits. qc_duplicate_calls.py reports no EXACT duplicates: the one it found during the build, two Serpentovirinae accessory orthogroups calling each other's loci at identical coordinates although blastp finds no HSP between their collections even at e = 10, was fixed by merging them into one feature with a profile per orthogroup. check_met_start.py is clean, after one 224 aa bovine torovirus M deposit beginning WPFPDQ — an unmarked partial inside the literature's own length window — was dropped along with the single-sequence profile built from it.

07What these modules cannot do

Written from the measurements. Most of it is the literature's limit rather than the module's.

gapwhy, and why tuning will not close it
No mature peptides at all in Serpentovirinae and RemotovirinaeNo cleavage site of any kind is published for either subfamily, and no protease from either has ever been expressed or assayed. A PubMed search for serpentovirus or bostovirus protease work returns nothing. The modules stop at the polyprotein, as the source-annotation ceiling requires.
Only one mature peptide in Torovirinae and PiscanivirinaeThirteen nsps are described for Torovirus, but only the main proteinase's own two boundaries are Edman-confirmed. The other eleven EToV sites are predicted from the FxxQ↓(S,A) consensus, and the ten non-self porcine torovirus sites were validated in a surrogate biosensor rather than mapped in the authentic polyprotein. Predicted coordinates are not shipped as if they were measured.
No S1/S2 subunitsTorovirus S is cleaved into two subunits and that is experimental (PMID 2219698), but the scissile bond has never been mapped; the “furin site” in the review literature is a motif assignment. Literature coordinates or nothing.
No signal peptidesNo signal-peptidase site has been mapped in any tobanivirus. The bostovirus S site at residues 18/19 is a prediction.
ORF1ab is called on 62–88% of panel genomesNot a threshold problem. Of the eleven panel misses, seven are genomes carrying ambiguous bases, where the annotator demotes the call to partial_cds by design because the gap-fill draws its bases from the subject; two are 5′-truncated contigs with no pp1a at all; two have no reference inside the 95% identity gate. The same ambiguity is why those genomes are not references in the first place, so the two populations coincide.
Torovirus U1 and U2 are not modelledThey are real, conserved, under purifying selection and efficiently translated (PMID 29950409) — and CUG-initiated. The annotator scans to a Met start, and the whole dump holds one deposit of each.
Two lineages have no moduleGlycera tridactyla and Salmo salar tobaniviruses, one genome each, 28% and 42% replicase identity to anything else in the family.
Most profiles rest on one sequenceA consequence of the sampling, not of the method: 149 of 176 profiles are single-sequence. The fix is more genomes, not more tuning.

The three kept sp. isolates are a curator's decision, not a measurement. 125 records whose organism name is <taxon> sp. were dropped at intake, per the standing rule. Three were kept because BV-BRC gives them real ICTV species labels — Sertovirus cona, Septovirus foka, Infratovirus latu — while putting sp. in the isolate name; each is the only genome of its genus in the dump, so dropping them would delete three of the seven serpentovirus genera. That decision is flagged in the build notes for review.

And one number on this page is not what it appears to be. run_gto_eval.py counts a feature as called when its annotation string appears in the GTO. Nine Serpentovirinae features share the string Uncharacterized lineage-specific protein, so that tool reports an identical rate for all nine. Every per-feature rate quoted here is computed from the feature tables, which carry the gene symbol.

"Near-complete" is measured against the genome each module expects rather than a family-wide constant — a bostovirus is 20 kb and a veiled chameleon serpentovirus 36 kb — so an exemplar counts as near-complete when it reaches 90% of the median length of its own module's reference genomes (Torovirinae 28,511 nt, Piscanivirinae 27,004, Remotovirinae 20,386, Serpentovirinae 30,104). 49 of the 117 exemplars clear that; the other 68 are partial submissions with a median length of 4,731 nt, and 90.5% of them are correctly flagged incomplete, which is the quality script doing its job rather than a module shortfall.

36 of the 49 near-complete exemplars were annotated against the module as it ships; the other 13 hit the same ID-server 403. All 36 routed, 30 came out good (83.3%), and the six that did not are named: four carry an ORF1a polyprotein: Feature is too short flag (a divergent genome whose polyprotein matches partially — the profile sets are thin, not the genomes), one is missing N, and Bangali torovirus carries Feature is too long on ORF1a. Every one is a profile-coverage statement about a divergent lineage, and every one is visible rather than silent.

The Serpentovirinae row is the one to read twice. Most of its near-complete exemplars are the metagenomic sp. contigs dropped at intake, so they are in no collection and are not transcript-edit references — which is exactly why ORF1ab falls to 44% there, the correction gate needing a reference within 95% identity. The four structural features are still called on 100% of them. Those profiles are reaching genomes that contributed nothing to them.

One defect the whole-taxon run found, and it was a cutoff. On Berne virus a second Membrane glycoprotein was called at position 11,322, inside the replicase ORF, scoring 30.34 bits against a cutoff of 30 — while the genuine M on the same genome scored 476.8. The generator derives a cutoff from the profile, and for a 233-residue protein that lands at 30, which is not a threshold. Every feature's cutoff was reset to half the lowest score its own panel calls actually reached, capped at 500 so a 4,500-residue polyprotein does not exclude the divergent genomes it is there to find. The false call is gone and no genuine call was lost.

Routing is complete, the structural proteins are called on essentially every near-complete genome that carries them, and every shortfall on this page has a named cause rather than a threshold left to tune.

Rarefied over proteins rather than genomes: at the depth both curves reach (339 proteins) the source vocabulary holds 65 distinct strings and the modules 9. The source curve is still climbing at 368 proteins; the controlled one saturates after 45, because the Nth protein reuses a name already seen.

The collapse is visible one protein at a time. The membrane protein arrives as membrane protein (141), membrane glycoprotein (70), M (20), M protein (2), membrance protein (1, a typo) and envelope protein (1) — six strings, one protein, one annotation. The spike arrives as seven, including peplomer protein (AA 1 to 1562). Nine of the fourteen Serpentovirinae features share one string, Uncharacterized lineage-specific protein, which is correct rather than lazy: the literature gives none of them a function.

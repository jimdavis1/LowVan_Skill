Does the human parechovirus module work?

Parechovirus is the genus of human parechovirus, which infects most children before school age and is a leading cause of sepsis-like illness and meningitis in infants under three months. The genus also holds parechoviruses of rodents, ferrets, cattle and bats. This audit covers the module built for it: what it covers, how it was built, where the cutoffs sit, whether genomes reach it, whether it calls the proteins, and what it cannot do.

01What the module covers

One genus, eleven features, and a capsid that is cleaved one time fewer than most picornaviruses.

The parechovirus polyprotein is VP0, VP3, VP1, 2A, 2B, 2C, 3A, VPg, 3C, 3D. There is no leader, and VP0 is never cleaved into VP4 and VP2, so the mature capsid carries three proteins rather than four. That is the organisational difference that puts this genus and Avihepatovirus in a different group from Enterovirus and Hepatovirus, which cleave VP0.

25 rep contigs ship with the module; 23 of them are the best BLASTn hit for at least one exemplar. Routing was measured against the ten Picornaviridae modules only — nucleotide BLASTn cannot reach the routing threshold across virus families, and leaving the other 56 modules' 558 rep contigs out cut the run time four-fold.

The one genome that does not route is 3413225.3, Bos parechovirus 1 NZ1, at 6,134 nucleotides. It is the only bovine parechovirus in the set, so no reference is close enough to claim it at the nucleotide level. A twenty-sixth reference would fix exactly this one genome, which is the kind of marginal gain the 25-reference budget exists to decline.

07What it cannot do

The bat parechovirus 3A, and one bovine genome that never arrives.

3A is not called on any bat parechovirus. All twelve bat genomes route and get their other ten features, so the module reaches them and reads them; it is 3A specifically that no profile covers. 3A is the shortest of the non-VPg features here, and short proteins are where sequence divergence costs the most, because there is less of the protein over which a profile can accumulate score. Whether a profile built from bat 3A sequences would separate cleanly from the human one has not been measured, and the collection contains no bat 3A to build from.

One genome does not route at all. Bos parechovirus 1 NZ1 is the single bovine parechovirus in BV-BRC and no rep contig claims it. A genome that does not route is never annotated however good the profiles are, so this is a routing gap rather than a profile gap, and it costs one genome.

Eight human parechoviruses also miss 3A. Those are scattered across a species with 202 exemplars and are more likely to be individual divergent isolates or partial assemblies than a lineage the profiles systematically miss. They have not been examined one by one.

The module calls all eleven proteins on the human parechoviruses, which are what the genus is medically about, and misses 3A on every bat parechovirus. Closing that needs bat 3A sequences in the collection, which BV-BRC does not currently annotate; it is not a threshold that can be lowered.

08What the run found

Nothing needed fixing.

Full-taxon coverage and the held-out panel were both run for the first time on 5 October 2026. No feature trailed the best-called one by 20 points, the panel returned no flags at all, and the module has not been rebuilt since it was built on 2 October.

The bat 3A gap is visible only in the per-species breakdown of the full-taxon run. At the genus level 3A is called on 89% of exemplars, which reads as ordinary variation; it is only when the misses turn out to be 12 of 12 bat genomes that it becomes a statement about what the module covers.

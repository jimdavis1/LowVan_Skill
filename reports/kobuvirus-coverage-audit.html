Does the kobuvirus module work?

Kobuvirus is the genus of Aichivirus A, a cause of gastroenteritis in people, together with the bovine and porcine kobuviruses of veterinary importance and a long tail of rodent, bat and bird viruses. Forty-five species in one module. This audit covers what it includes, how it was built, whether genomes reach it, whether it calls the proteins, and what it cannot do.

01What the module covers

One genus, 45 species, and an uncleaved VP0.

The kobuvirus polyprotein is a leader, VP0, VP3, VP1, 2A, 2B, 2C, 3A, VPg, 3C and 3D. VP0 is never cleaved into VP4 and VP2, so the mature capsid carries three proteins rather than four — the same arrangement as Parechovirus and Avihepatovirus, and the reason those genera group apart from Enterovirus and Hepatovirus.

07What it cannot do

The leader in the one- and two-genome species.

26 exemplars miss the leader, and they concentrate in the smallest species. It is missing from 3 of 3 Kobuvirus ecuni, 3 of 3 K. femyomini, 2 of 2 grey squirrel kobuviruses and 5 of 15 genomes labelled only Kobuvirus sp. — against 2 of 146 for K. cebes and 2 of 49 for K. aichi. A species represented by two or three genomes contributes almost nothing to a collection, so its leader is covered only to the extent that it resembles the well-sequenced ones.

The leader is where this shows first because it is the least conserved feature. Every other protein in the genome is called on 97% or more of exemplars including those same small species, so the module reaches them and reads them; it is the leader specifically that diverges faster than the profiles follow.

Projection did not change this. It roughly doubled every collection, including the leader's, and the leader call rate did not move. That is informative rather than disappointing: the sequences projection adds come from genomes that already had a close reference, so it deepens coverage of the populations already represented and does not extend it to new ones. Where a gap is caused by a lineage being absent rather than thin, more data of the same kind does not close it.

2A and VP3 were deliberately left out of the 6 October projection rebuild, because the source annotation contradicts itself. Counting the residues either side of every annotated start, the same genomic position carries two incompatible conventions:
VP3  LRYVAP|QHWKT  61
VP3  RHVTKQ|HWKTR  46
2A   FIVKVQ|RPTYV   3
2A   AQRPTY|VHWAL  55
VP3 differs by one residue, 2A by five.

Pooled, that reads as an unconserved cleavage site — P1 'P 53%' for VP3 and 'Q 48%' for 2A — and the site test refused both. That is the correct outcome: a projected cut inherits whichever convention the nearest reference happened to use, so projecting would have mixed both forms into one collection. The minority form is the right one in each case, since 3C cleaves after P1, which puts VP3 at H and 2A at R. Normalising the majority to the motif would make both projectable but rewrites boundaries on 116 source-annotated genomes, so it is a curation decision and was not taken here. Both features keep their full collections (VP3 116, 2A 96) and are unaffected at runtime.

Three genera with no module of their own are annotated by this one. Ludopivirus routes here on 7 of 7 exemplars at 10.1 features per genome, Sakobuvirus on 4 of 5 at 9.8, and Rabovirus on 9 of 12. None is declared by this module; all three were measured on 6 October across the 404 genomes ≥6 kb of the 36 unclaimed genera.

Every exemplar routes and eleven of twelve features are called on 97% or more. The leader is called on 92% and the misses are concentrated in species represented by two or three genomes each. That is a sampling limit in BV-BRC rather than a tunable threshold, and the rebuild demonstrated it directly: doubling the collection did not move the number.

08What the run found

The rebuild deepened the module without widening it.

Projection added 18 profiles and roughly doubled every collection, and the coverage figures barely moved: 278 complete genomes before and after, the leader at 287 both times. Taken with Sapelovirus, where the same technique moved a feature from 6 calls to 170, and Hepatovirus, where it moved the complete count by five, this is the clearest statement of what the technique does.

Projection carries cut coordinates from an annotated genome to a close relative. It therefore converts unannotated genomes into training data, which helps enormously when a feature is annotated in almost no genome at all — Sapelovirus had eleven leaders — and helps very little when a feature is already represented and the gap is a lineage no reference comes within 50% identity of. Kobuvirus was in the second case and the measurement says so.

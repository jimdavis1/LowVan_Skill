01What the module calls

02Where it does not call, and why

REM's overall rate is low and the denominator is the reason. Rem exists in the MMTV clade and nowhere else in the genus, and it fires in every MMTV-group genome of the held-out panel — 100% inside the clade. Its overlap with ENV is real MMTV architecture, since rem shares the envelope signal-peptide exon, and is declared expected rather than suppressed. REM is also the clearest case in this family of a feature the strings could never have found: zero deposits anywhere in the genus name it.

04Vocabulary saturation

How fast each vocabulary stops growing as genomes are added.

The deposited vocabulary keeps growing; the module's does not. At the depth both reach, 38.8 distinct source strings correspond to 5.0 controlled annotations, a 7.8-fold collapse. The module reaches 95% of its final vocabulary after 52 proteins against the source's 259.

05Method

Coverage is measured by annotating genomes through the real GTO pipeline — annotate_by_viral_pssm-GTO.pl, then the transcript-edit and splice passes, then viral_genome_quality.pl — not by a reimplementation. Spliced features are included, which the flat feature-table path cannot represent. Exemplars are clustered at 95% nucleotide identity over 90% of length, so one row is one distinct sequence rather than one deposit: this module's 145 genomes reduce to 53 exemplars, and without that step a rate would report how often a sequence was deposited rather than how often the module calls it. Call rates are taken over the 52 of 53 exemplars this module actually claimed; an unrouted genome contributes no features and counting it would understate every rate, and this is the same denominator the evaluation and the copy_num decisions used.

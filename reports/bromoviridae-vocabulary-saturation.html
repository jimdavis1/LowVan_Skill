Bromoviridae

A controlled vocabulary stops growing. Free text does not.

A Bromoviridae genome encodes the same 5 modelled proteins as any other, so a curated vocabulary needs exactly 5 names no matter how many genomes you read. Free-text product strings have no such ceiling: each new submitter spells the same proteins a new way. Sampling 147 annotated genomes 100 times over shows the two curves doing exactly that — the module is saturated after 2 genomes, while the source vocabulary is still climbing at 147.

The ratio is the number worth quoting: at the end of each curve, free text costs 33.3 strings per 100 genomes and the controlled vocabulary 3.4 — a 9.8-fold collapse. Reaching 95% of its final size takes the source vocabulary 132 genomes (90% of the set); the module gets there on genome 2.

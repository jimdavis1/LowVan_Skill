The reason this module exists

Allexivirus TGB3 initiates at a CUG, not an AUG.

Lezzhov et al. 2015, J Gen Virol 96:3159–64 (PMID 26296665) showed by site-directed mutagenesis that shallot virus X — the type species — translates TGB3 from a CUG by leaky scanning, on a bicistronic template shared with TGB2. The coding sequence is conserved across the genus; only the initiator is non-canonical.

Three observations in the sequence data were originally read as evidence that allexivirus TGB3 does not exist. The paper explains all three, and the gene is real:

The correction matters for how the module is built. Suppressing the calls — by raising bit_cutoff or retiring the profile — would have removed a real gene. Both were measured and neither worked anyway: the score distributions overlap (Allexivirus 31.4–71.6, Potexvirus 44.9–202.3, so no cutoff separates them), and with the dominant profile retired 75 of 82 genomes still received a TGB3 from the next profiles down.

TGB3 at 2 against 190 is the row that could not be reconciled with Potexvirus, where the same feature scores 38 against 10. Turning the scan off stops the annotator hunting an AUG that does not exist and emitting a CDS ~35 residues too long into TGB2.

The 5′ coordinate of TGB3 is approximate, and that is a known limitation.

upstream_ext: 0 stops the over-extension but does not place the N-terminus on the true CUG, because the CUG cannot be located mechanically: 65% of genomes have some in-frame CTG upstream of the match but at 1–47 codons with no consistent offset, and 35% have none before a stop. The call is therefore anchored at the homology boundary. min_len 40 and max_len 130 are deliberately wide — a gate tightened to the training distribution would flag the coordinate rather than the genome.

Routing

25 references cover 303 of 306 genomes with contigs: 99.0%, the highest of any module built for this class. Three clusters holding three genomes remain uncovered. The genus has a few well-sequenced dominant species — the garlic viruses A, B, C, D, E and X plus shallot virus X — which is precisely the structure that makes routing cheap, in contrast to Endornaviridae where 141 species formed 141 nucleotide islands and the taxon was withdrawn.

Known weaknesses

  • TGB3 rests on three training sequences, all isolates of one virus (Allexivirus sigmamedicagonis). Two further Allexivirus TGB3 proteins exist in the collection — from Blackberry calico virus and Garlic virus C AS16 — but never clustered. The profile generalises across the genus, but the evidence base is thin and the N-terminus is unresolved.
  • NABP is called on 65% of genomes. Whether the remaining third genuinely lack it or the profiles miss them is unmeasured.
  • copy_num cannot distinguish "expected once" from "required". viral_genome_quality.pl treats any CDS carrying copy_num as essential, so a genus-restricted accessory can have neither — which is why duplicate ALLEXI_40K calls cannot be flagged without falsely flagging every genome that lacks it.

Provenance

76 profiles against 76 alignments, no feature mismatched, no ragged alignments. Every profile was selected from the parent module on the criterion that ≥80% of its training sequences are Allexivirus; 75 of 76 are 100% Allexivirus and the remaining one (TGB1.9) is 95%. Profiles were not deleted from the parent, following the Merhavirus precedent.

The Lezzhov citation sits in PMID_claude_generated, not PMID. It was found and read only as an abstract and secondary sources by a language model; a curator has not confirmed it, and reading a paper does not clear the flag.

07What it cannot do

One documented architecture note and one genuinely open question.

ORF2a and ORF5a are called entirely inside TGB1 and CP — 100% of their calls. That is the architecture their names describe: ORF2a is an alternative reading frame overlapping ORF2, ORF5a overlaps ORF5. The collections are unrelated to their hosts (28% and 31% identity over about a quarter coverage), neither is essential, and nothing about this is a defect.

P14 is a different matter and is NOT resolved. It is called entirely within the CP span on 100% of its 91 calls, and its collection is unrelated to CP (33% identity over 33% coverage) — so unlike the two alternative-frame ORFs, the name does not account for the overlap. p14 is the nucleic-acid-binding protein and should sit downstream of the coat protein. Either CP's called span runs long (its window reaches 421 aa, high for this group) or the profile is promiscuous at bit_cutoff 30. It is called on 60.7% of genomes and carries no copy_num, so no essentiality or quality figure depends on it — but it is an open question and is recorded as one on the feature itself.

The evaluation is a sample — 150 genomes at ≥6 kb, seed 29. Routing in §04 is a census.

08What the run found

No corrections, and one thing worth returning to.

This module's copy_num was already right, which puts it with Carlavirus and Ampelovirus rather than with Velarivirus and Closterovirus. The P14 nesting is the only finding, and the honest status is unresolved rather than fixed: diagnosing it properly means deciding whether the CP length window or the P14 cutoff is at fault, and that is a change to a shipped module on the strength of one measurement.

Eight of eight features have profiles and five are essential on measurement. P14's nesting is documented and open; nothing depends on it.

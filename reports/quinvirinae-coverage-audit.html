07What it cannot do

Two documented architecture notes, one closed question, and one number still to reproduce.

ORF2a and ORF5a are called entirely inside TGB1 and CP — 100% of their calls. That is the architecture their names describe: ORF2a is an alternative reading frame overlapping ORF2, ORF5a overlaps ORF5. The collections are unrelated to their hosts (28% and 31% identity over about a quarter coverage), neither is essential, and nothing about this is a defect.

P14 is closed, 30 September 2026. Where it is called it sits inside the CP span — 92.5% and 89.6% containment on the two GRSPaV rep contigs, above the 80% floor that keeps the duplicate-call check from crying wolf over the routine short overlaps of a compact genome. It is not a profile calling someone else's gene. The two features share no detectable homology in either direction: blastp over 170 P14 × 915 CP collection members returns zero HSPs at E ≤ 1e-3; the best P14 profile scores 21.7 bits against a CP member and the best CP profile 22.4 bits against a P14 member, against cutoffs of 30 and 80; and the two proteins actually emitted at that locus — a 259 aa nucleocapsid protein and a 125 aa 14 kDa protein — align nowhere at all, not even at E ≤ 1000. What the coordinates show instead is an out-of-frame overlapping ORF: on ON868740, CP at 7847–8623 in frame +2 and p14 at 8277–8651 in frame +3; on KT000366, 7771–8547 (+2) and 8228–8584 (+3). Real variation, one protein encoded overlapping another, accepted by the curator as such. Neither feature is dropped and neither profile set is rebuilt; qc_duplicate_calls.py takes --expected P14:CP for this module. Nothing depends on it either way, because P14 carries no copy_num.

ORF2a nested in TGB1 is closed too, on the same measurement. On KT000366 the annotator calls ORF2a at 6819–6962 in frame +3, entirely inside TGB1 at 6578–7240 in frame +2 — 100% containment. The two-direction test gives the same answer as P14:CP: blastp over 95 ORF2A × 654 TGB1 collection members returns zero HSPs at E ≤ 1e-3, the best cross-profile score is 22.2 bits in either direction against cutoffs of 30 and 60, and the two emitted proteins — 221 aa and 43 aa — align nowhere at E ≤ 1000. qc_duplicate_calls.py takes --expected P14:CP,ORF2A:TGB1 for this module. Two real out-of-frame overlapping ORFs in one eight-feature module is a property of this genome organisation, not a pattern to assume elsewhere.

The earlier reading of this overlap was wrong and is worth recording as such. This section previously called it unexplained and offered two candidate causes — that CP's called span runs long, or that the P14 profile is promiscuous at bit_cutoff 30. Neither is the case. The frames settle it, and no window or cutoff needs touching. The inference that a nested call must mean one profile is misbehaving is the thing to distrust: in a compact viral genome it can equally mean the genes genuinely overlap, and only a homology measurement in both directions distinguishes the two.

P14's call rate is unreconciled and should be re-measured. §05's table reports 60.7% of 150 routed genomes (91 calls). The only measurement on disk is a 108-genome quality run, which gives 22.2% (24 calls) — cross-checked against that run's feature-count distribution, which accounts for all 24 exactly. The two disagree on ORF5a as well, 8.7% against 25.9%, so this is not a rounding difference and one of them is measuring a different population or is simply wrong. The 150-genome output is not on disk to check. The closure above does not rest on the rate — it rests on the frames and the absence of homology — but the rate should not be quoted from either source until one is reproduced.

One thing the on-disk run does show clearly is that the accessories are nearly mutually exclusive: 52 genomes carry the five core features and nothing else, 22 carry core + P14, 24 carry core + ORF2a + ORF5a, and exactly one carries all three. That is two accessory architectures inside one module rather than a gradient, and it is the kind of split that a single pooled call rate hides.

The evaluation is a sample — 150 genomes at ≥6 kb, seed 29. Routing in §04 is a census.

The homology rescue has now run on this module. Completed 1 October 2026, closing the item deferred on 30 September. rescue_unassigned.py takes every protein no annotation-string rule matched, blastp’s it against this module’s own collections and adopts each into the feature it matches at ≥80% identity over ≥60% of the query, refusing everything below that rather than sweeping it into a grab-bag. It had never run on this module because it crashed on the trailing tab BV-BRC writes into uniq.seq. 664 of 836 unassigned sequences were adopted; the training set went 4026 → 4374 sequences (+9%) and the profile set 116 → 115. Four genomes gained, none lost, and one profile fewer — the added sequences merged clusters that had been separate rather than creating new ones, which is what more training data is supposed to do. ORF2A and ORF5A gained nothing at all, correctly: assign_accessories.py had already claimed that pool by homology. 280 partial replicases were parked in REP.outliers.fasta and were not used for profile building. Measured against a control — the shipped module scored on the same 108 genomes with the current JSON — the rescue moved genuinely-clean from 79.6% to 83.3% (4 genome(s) improved, 0 regressed). The earlier comparison against measurements/quality.tsv was discarded: that table predates the 30 September copy_num and vocabulary edits, which on some modules dominate the rescue entirely.

08What the run found

No corrections, the open question closed, and one measurement to redo.

This module's copy_num was already right, which puts it with Carlavirus and Ampelovirus rather than with Velarivirus and Closterovirus. The P14 nesting was the only finding and it is now resolved rather than merely documented: measured in both directions, shown to be an out-of-frame overlapping ORF, and closed by the curator as real variation. No feature was dropped and no profile set was rebuilt. P14 carries no copy_num, so genomes without it are not flagged for missing it — quality2.tsv reports zero ‘missing essential feature: 14 kDa protein’ against 84 in the pre-correction quality.tsv. The same architecture appears once more in the module: ORF2a is called 100% inside TGB1, out of frame, on KT000366, and is closed on the same measurement and the same reasoning. One measurement is left open: P14's call rate, which §05 and the on-disk quality run do not agree on.

Eight of eight features have profiles and five are essential on measurement. P14's nesting is measured, explained and closed — an out-of-frame overlapping ORF, no homology with CP in either direction, nothing depending on it. Its call rate is the one number here still to be reproduced.

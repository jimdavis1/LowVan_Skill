01What the module covers

One genus, one module, and nothing about the genome organisation is in doubt.

Seadornaviruses are mosquito-borne. Banna virus is the one that matters clinically — it has been isolated from patients with encephalitis in China, Vietnam and Indonesia — and the other three species are known mainly from mosquito pools.

Parts-per-name peaks at exactly 12 across 44 strain names, which is both the ICTV segment count and the evidence that the grouping key used to reassemble these genomes is right. Every record in this family's BV-BRC dump is a single segment; the routing and coverage figures below are measured on 46 genomes reassembled from records sharing a strain name and contributing exactly twelve segments.

This is a small module. 1,202 protein features, 793 unique sequences, 696 of them in a collection — against 48,007 for Rotavirus. Banna virus supplies roughly half. Every number below should be read with that in mind.

Every other synonym was tested against the length distribution of the sequences carrying it, the procedure this project adopted after the Orbivirus build, and all but one covered more than one protein: nonstructural protein sits on 41 records matching no feature at all and 29 matching VP11; outer capsid protein on VP12 24 times and VP10 3.

RNA-dependent RNA polymerase failed the test again. On 12 records it sits on VP1 six times, VP2 once, and three times on something matching no feature. Orbivirus gave the same result. Twice is a pattern, so this project now never binds that string by rule in any taxon, even though it names the polymerase wherever one exists: a few deposits always put it on the wrong protein, and the homology rescue can separate them per sequence where a rule cannot.

07What it cannot do

What this module cannot do.

It is built on 696 sequences. That is the whole limitation and everything else follows from it. Eleven features at 100% on a held-out panel is the easiest result in this family, and it should be read as this module describes its own dump well rather than as a claim about seadornaviruses in general.

Mangshi virus contributes 36 records to the entire genus. Its profiles rest on very few independent observations, and self-recall cannot distinguish a profile that generalises from one that has memorised four training sequences. The same caution applies in weaker form to Liao ning and Kadipiro.

VP5 and VP6 are not cleanly separable outside Banna virus. Both run about 505 residues in the other three species and cross-match each other's profiles. The annotator's best-hit rule resolves it in practice — both are called in 100% of the panel — but the margin is thinner than the numbers suggest, and a genuinely novel seadornavirus could land on the wrong one.

Reassortment is not modelled. Segments are annotated independently.

Complete for the genus as deposited, with twelve features called in essentially every genome. The honest caveat is size rather than any specific gap: this is a 696-sequence module, and its perfect scores are easier to earn than Rotavirus's.

08What the run found

What the run turned up that was not expected.

The contamination audit reported 99 moves — 14% of the module — and every one of them was wrong. The tool compared each sequence to one canonical per feature, taken from that feature's largest cluster. Banna virus is half the dump, so it won every largest-cluster, and all twelve canonicals were Banna. The Kadipiro, Liao ning and Mangshi proteins then matched whichever Banna canonical was nearest.

The symmetry is what gave it away: VP5→VP6 24 and VP6→VP5 24, equal in both directions. Real contamination runs one way, because it comes from mislabelled deposits. Applying those moves would have shuffled three species' proteins at random. The tool now takes one reference per cluster rather than per feature, so a divergent species is judged against its own lineage wherever one exists; the count drops to 8, and all 8 verify by length and margin.

Only 0.0% of this dump declares itself partial, matching Orbivirus and against 39% for Rotavirus. The difference is deposit practice: rotavirus VP4 and VP7 are genotyping targets routinely sequenced in part, while seadornavirus deposits are whole segments.

01What the module covers

One module for a whole subfamily — seven genera that share a genome layout but not much else.

The module covers Chaphamaparvovirus, Chapparvovirus, Brevihamaparvovirus, Hepanhamaparvovirus, Penstylhamaparvovirus, Ichthamaparvovirus and Ichtchaphamaparvovirus. They are held together because the layout is the same and their peripheral profiles do not cross-match, which is the test for whether a set of genera is one module or several.

Five features are declared: NS1, NS2, NS3, NP1 and VP1. NS1 and VP1 carry copy_num and are the two the quality score requires. NS2, NS3 and NP1 are present in some genera and absent in others, which is why none of them is marked essential.

Sixty distinct annotation strings collapse onto the five feature names. VP1 draws on twenty of them and NS1 on fifteen. Some of the collapses are not spelling at all but naming: NP1 is assembled from nonstructural protein 2, NP protein, NP and nucleoprotein among others, and NS3 from nonstructural protein 3, accessory protein p15 and nonstructural protein p15.

That NP1 list is worth pausing on. NP1 and NS2 are slots in a genome layout, not names of proteins, and different submitters put different proteins in them. The binning here is by sequence, not by the slot a deposit was filed under.

04Does it route?

Routing is a BLASTn of the query against the reference contigs, so a genome with no close relative among them is invisible to the module — and a genome whose closest relative sits in another module routes there instead.

Routing was measured against the 25 reference contigs this module ships, of which 24 were used by the exemplar set.

Of 108 exemplars, 95 routed to this module, 10 routed to another, and 3 did not route at all. Eight of the ten went to DensovirinaeMono and two to Protoparvovirus.

Those eight are not a surprise once you read their names: Aedes aegypti densovirus 1, Aedes albopictus C6/36 cell densovirus, Anopheles gambiae densovirus and four Penaeus monodon hepandensovirus isolates. They are Brevihamaparvovirus and Hepanhamaparvovirus by current taxonomy, but they sit beside Brevidensovirus and Hepandensovirus on the tree, and routing is nucleotide identity, not taxonomy. The two that went to Protoparvovirus are chaphamaparvoviruses from waterfowl and matched there on NP1 alone.

On the 297-genome panel the same split holds: 280 routed here, 15 to DensovirinaeMono, 2 to Protoparvovirus.

279 of 297 come through with no genome-level and no contig-level quality flag. The flags that do appear are concentrated: NS1 called twice in 11 genomes, VP1 twice in 3, a contig with more than 1% ambiguous bases in 3, and a missing essential capsid in 4.

A feature called twice is the module finding two acceptable matches in one genome and keeping both. In this subfamily that usually means a genome assembled with a duplicated terminal region, not a second gene — but the audit does not distinguish those two cases, so the count is reported rather than explained away.

Read the headline figure for what it is. The panel still contains the genomes the reference contigs were taken from, so it measures internal consistency, not out-of-sample behaviour.

07What it cannot do

Four limits, each measured rather than supposed.

Routing is the weakest link, and it is weakest exactly where the taxonomy is newest. Thirteen of 108 exemplars either misrouted or did not route. The subfamily was carved out of the densoviruses recently, and the reference contigs of the neighbouring modules still sit closer to some of its members than its own do.

No splice features are declared. Nothing in the collections showed the two-exon signature that put NS2 and NS3 into the splice caller in Amdoparvovirus and Protoparvovirus. If a spliced product exists in these genera, this module does not call it and would not report its absence.

NS2, NS3 and NP1 are clade-specific and are named as such. Two of them carry the word uncharacterized in their function, which is the honest state: the sequences cluster and are callable, and what they do is not established in the literature for most of these genera.

The module is bounded by what BV-BRC annotated. Eight strings, 18 sequences in all, did not collapse onto any feature and are tracked in UNASSIGNED_TRACKING.tsv, among them nonstructural protein p10 and accessory protein p10 in Chaphamaparvovirus. At three and two occurrences they are below the member floor for declaring a feature, but they are not nothing.

Use this module for hamaparvovirus genomes that route to it. Check the routing first: one exemplar in eight went somewhere else or nowhere, and a genome that routes to DensovirinaeMono will be annotated with that module's features, not these.

08What the run found

What the build turned up that was not expected.

The densovirus names are the finding. Eight exemplars whose deposited names say densovirus or hepandensovirus are hamaparvoviruses under current taxonomy, and they route to the densovirus module anyway. The names are not wrong so much as older than the subfamily, and nothing in a build that trusts the deposit would catch it.

The practical consequence is in the sample, not the module: a Brevihamaparvovirus genome used as training data here is also the nearest neighbour of a DensovirinaeMono reference contig, so the two modules compete for it at routing time.
